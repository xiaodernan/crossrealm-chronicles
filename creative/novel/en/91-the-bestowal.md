# The Unchanted Chronicle of Crossrealm: The Keeper of the Anchor Fire

**Genre**: Planar fantasy / dark epic / metafiction
**Adapted from**: Crossrealm Chronicles
**Volume XIII**: New Fire — the Seven-Hundred Year

## Ninety-one: The Bestowal

The kitchen bowl tipped itself into the sink, gently, by itself.

A-Shi had just finished the evening pot. His mother came out of the back room carrying two pairs of chopsticks and two clean bowls — glanced at the stove — then placed both in A-Shi's hands.

"Today's errand," she said quietly.

A-Shi looked at the bowls.

"The relay station at the mountain," his mother added, as though that needed no explanation. She handed him a small bundle woven tightly from coarse rice straw — something flat inside, no bigger than a man's palm.

"The fire waits for you," she said.

A-Shi understood before she finished speaking. He tucked the bundle against his chest. Xiao Huo was already at the doorway, waiting.

They walked up the old mountain road. It was spring; the first peach blossoms were open, and a warm morning wind carried their scent across the valley.

The relay station was at the far side of the ridge — a single-room, three window situation with one roof, one door, one stove. No plaque. No lock. The doorway faced south.

Baiyin sat in front of the relay station's stove. She was tending a tiny wooden plaque suspended inside the stove, watching it blacken and not burn.

A-Shi set the straw bundle down in front of her on the kitchen table.

He did not speak.

Baiyin reached over and slowly unraveled the straw twine.

The bundle opened.

Inside, there was nothing.

Pure white emptiness; a clean, perfect void; an empty box of flawless grain — a gift leaving its shape in the wood itself.

Baiyin smiled faintly at A-Shi.

"Sixty-first generation of keepers," she said.

"It is empty."

"It cannot be otherwise."

Xiao Huo looked at the floor.

"There is no fire in it at all," he said.

"That is the gift," Baiyin said gently. "For six hundred years, the fire has only ever been borrowed. Every keeper passed it on to the next. Today you returned it to the one who first carried it."

A-Shi asked, puzzled but listening:

"Then what is inside this one?"

"You poured it into the stove six hundred years ago," Baiyin said softly. "Or six hundred years ago the fire poured itself into you."

Xiao Huo looked at his shoes.

"After six hundred years," Baiyin said quietly, "the fire and its keepers have finally arrived at the relay station."

A-Shi listened.

He really listened.

"But Grandmother Bai does not live here," he said, turning to Baiyin. "I never saw her. Do you know where she is?"

Baiyin smiled.

She set the empty box back in front of him.

"The fire," she said, "is where it has always been."

Xiao Huo lifted his head.

"She is in the stove," he said breathlessly.

Baiyin nodded very slowly.

"Six hundred years," she said, "and it has carried you all this way until you could stand before it and still know its name."

A-Shi looked down at his hands.

"I never called it Grandmother Bai," he whispered.

"Every keeper never calls her by name," Baiyin said. "They only call her what they always knew."

Xiao Huo settled back against the relay station's threshold.

"Six hundred years," he said, "and all she ever wanted was for the stove to be kept."

Baiyin nodded.

They walked back together, all three of them — A-Shi, Xiao Huo, and Baiyin — down the mountain road, along the crest, back into the trees, until they emerged at the kitchen door at dusk.

The stove was burning brightly, as it always had been.

Xiao Huo stopped at the threshold and stepped forward.

A-Shi stopped at the threshold.

Baiyin remained outside, looking in.

Xiao Huo stepped forward and stood before the stove.

He pressed his open palm to the stove's side. The iron hummed under his hand.

"Grandmother," he said. "We are home."
