## Sixty-three: The Original Fire

Spring went on.

His mother went to the market early. The house was empty.

A-Shi wandered around. He opened every drawer. Finally, in the bottom drawer of the oldest wardrobe, was a small wooden chest.

He had never noticed it before.

He lifted the lid.

Inside were six small blackened iron plates. Each one bore a face.

He studied them one by one.

He recognized the features — they were all his grandfather. Every single one.

Different ages, different fringes, different expressions — but the same face.

He put them back into the chest as his mother returned home. He shut the lid quietly.

"What did you find?" she asked.

A-Shi hesitated.

"Nothing," he said.

His mother approached. She looked carefully at his face, saw something in it, and walked past him to the stove without another word.

"Will you make dinner?" she asked.

"Of course."

He followed her into the kitchen.

They cooked side by side. The silence between them was unbroken.

Finally A-Shi spoke.

"Then this is my family's fire token — isn't it?"

"Iron plaque?"

"Father?"

She didn't look up.

A-Shi lowered his eyes.

After a long moment, she said:

"I have never had a chance to tell you this. Someday you will make more."

"How long — do you know?"

"Six hundred years," she said.

A-Shi did not respond.

For a long time they sat beside the stove and watched. The fire was small, orange, and unhurried. The silence between them was simple and warm.

Six hundred years.

Six hundred years of fire — every generation reaching the next, one hand extended to the other.

He was in the middle now.

He was the center of a family.

He put the plates away in the wooden chest. He turned to the stove.

He thought about every person who had stood beside this same stove — his father, his grandfather, and uncounted more.

He put both hands closest to the fire.

His mother didn't turn.

"Just sit with it," she said.

He nodded.

He sat.

The fire burned steady and beautiful, forever.

He stayed until the stars appeared over the courtyard.

His grandfather — wherever he was — was warm.

A-Shi was exactly where he belonged.
