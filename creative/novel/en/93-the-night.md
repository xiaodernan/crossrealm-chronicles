# The Unchanted Chronicle of Crossrealm: The Keeper of the Anchor Fire

**Genre**: Planar fantasy / dark epic / metafiction
**Adapted from**: Crossrealm Chronicles
**Volume XIII**: New Fire — the Seven-Hundred Year

## Ninety-three: The Night

For the first time, Xiao Huo added the firewood himself.

His mother told him beforehand — not in words, but in the way she held out the bundle of split pine beside the stove. A-Shi did not intervene.

Xiao Huo took one piece, set it across the mouth of the stove, and let it go.

The fire swallowed it without a sound.

He stood very still, listening to the fire burn. After a while, when the coals had settled, he added a second piece.

The flames did not roar. They only brightened.

That night, as the household settled, A-Shi sat down at the hearth to rest. Xiao Huo sat beside him. Their mother dusted the kitchen. The house was quiet, steady, grounded.

An hour later a dry wind rose outside, brushing along the walls. The stove kept burning. The lights did not change.

Xiao Huo leaned closer to the stove. The heat came to meet his open palm.

Their mother brought a bowl of porridge from the stove's edge and set it between them.

She had noticed A-Shi drifting, one arm against his knee, his head drooping forward as if bone-deep asleep. She watched Xiao Huo reach out to steady him silently.

Xiao Huo was very small. He tapped A-Shi's shoulder lightly with one finger. A-Shi did not stir. He tapped again. A-Shi's eyes fluttered only.

Xiao Huo thought for a long moment.

He reached out, and touched the stove itself — fingertips against iron.

A hum quivered up through his hand — a low, almost inaudible vibration, like a heart in a deep, deep sleep.

That was how Grandmother Bai had always sounded in the stove. Xiao Huo had not known it until now.

He wrapped A-Shi in his own arms — small, thin arms, warm from the kitchen's everyday sun — and pressed his back against the stove's warm iron ledge.

A-Shi smiled in his sleep.

He said something brief and quiet, as though speaking to someone not present.

Xiao Huo whispered into the warm kitchen:

"Grandmother."

The stove answered — a faint tremor rose from the deepest layer of the flames.

Xiao Huo understood.

The stove had always been here. But someone had been holding it, all this time, from six hundred years away.

Baiyin. A-Shi's grandmother.

The fire had been walking beside the family all the while.

He sat a long time beside the stove. The dawn came slowly. The wind outside had settled. Outside, the fire-seed tree swayed faintly in what little breeze remained.

When his mother woke, the stove was burning higher than ever.

Xiao Huo had been sitting there all night. He was not tired. It was as though the fire had given him something to hold onto.

His mother took one look at him and understood everything.

She brought a bowl of rice porridge to Baiyin, who had come and gone quietly while they slept.

He was already up.

She set the bowl down before the stove.

The fire did not flicker.

She did not ask him anything.

Six hundred years of tending had never once let the family go.

The fire had never stopped.

A-Shi shifted against the stove and slept on.
