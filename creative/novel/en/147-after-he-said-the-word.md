## One Hundred and Forty-seven: After He Said the Word

Monday of the twenty-fifth week after the spring equinox — evening — the sky fully dark.

A-Shi stood at the stove — he did not move — he did not speak — he only watched the fire.

His mother made one motion — she pushed her own bowl across to him — she did not speak — A-Shi did not move the bowl — he did not lift his head.

His mother went out — she came back — she carried clean bowls — she set them on the table — she said nothing.

A-Shi slowly walked over — he looked at his mother — he looked at the fire — the fire burned.

His mother looked at A-Shi — she came over — she softly patted his shoulder from behind — he did not move.

The fire burned.

His mother said: “It has burned seven hundred years.”

A-Shi did not answer — the fire burned.

His mother said: “It burned seven hundred years — it burned your grandfather — and it burned your mother’s mother — and it will burn you too — it will keep burning.”

The fire burned.

A-Shi said: “I know.”

His mother nodded — she said nothing more — the fire burned.

The two of them looked at the fire — the fire burned.

A-Shi slowly squatted down — he pressed his face into his knees — he did not cry — he did not speak.

His mother squatted at the fire too — the fire burned.

The two of them just squatted there — the fire burned.

The fire burned.
