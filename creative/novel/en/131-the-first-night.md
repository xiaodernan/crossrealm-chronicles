## One Hundred and Thirty-one: The First Night

Saturday of the sixteenth week after the spring equinox — afternoon — the sky so clear it hurt to look up — the old keeper did not come out again.

Aunt Guo sent the porridge up first — the old keeper did not eat — no sound came from the back room at all.

A-Shi stood before the stove — the fire burned steadily. He was waiting for the old keeper. The old keeper did not come out. He waited a long time.

He squatted down and looked into the fire-box — the fire lit his face.

Uncle Zhou came and leaned against the door-post — he watched A-Shi.

No one said a single word.

When the light went, A-Shi fed the fire one birch branch — no more, no less — exactly a keeper's measure.

He sat down before the stove — he did not sleep — the fire stayed awake for him.

The next morning — the fire was still burning — A-Shi was still at the stove — the fire burned on.

The old keeper was in the back room — A-Shi heard one cough, once, from behind the door.

No one left.

Everyone in the town slowly understood — the old keeper wanted to hand the fire to A-Shi.

Nobody knew what was in the old keeper's heart.

The fire burned.

The fire burned.
