## One Hundred and Sixty-six: The Firekeeper

For seven days straight — A-Shi got up before dawn every day.

His mother noticed — the wood on the stove vanished fast — she said nothing.

On the eighth day — A-Shi walked to the stove in the dark — before it got light — he saw his mother sitting there — holding a cup of warm water — she was not looking at the fire — she was waiting for him.

A-Shi stopped at the door — he said: “Ma — why aren’t you asleep?”

His mother said: “I can’t sleep.”

A-Shi said: “I didn’t mean to wait for you —”

His mother shook her head — she said: “I know.”

The fire burned.

His mother said: “If you want to learn — I will teach you.”

A-Shi said: “Learn what?”

His mother said: “How to read the fire — when to add wood — when to hold back — seven hundred years ago, that is how they taught me.”

The fire burned.

A-Shi did not speak — the fire burned.

The fire burned.

The fire burned.
