## Twenty-five: Moonlit Corridor

Lin Wen walked a long time along the cobbled road.

Moonlight had fallen on the flagstones under the persimmon tree; he stepped onto one and found himself suddenly back at the first night — the night he first sat beneath the tree, his heart stumbling like the leaves themselves. When he came back to himself, he had already reached the end of the cobbled road — no maple forest there, no village silhouette, only a single mirror shining with moonlight.

He stood before the mirror for a long time.

The person inside the mirror was not him — a quite unfamiliar old man, hair white, robes so faded they were almost invisible. The old man's gaze never left his own feet, never once lifted.

Lin Wen stepped forward. His reflection in the mirror stepped forward as well.

The figure then fell back a step — or more precisely, it was as if one stranger had suddenly brushed against another's umbrella-edge. They stepped forward together. Then — the reflection stopped.

The old man lifted his head.

He was not an old man. He was another night of the same chronicle — the child the mirror had held, who had been standing on the other side for a very long time.

"You are not Lin Wen," the figure said softly.

Lin Wen did not immediately answer. He looked at his hands. He had always known this hour would come — perhaps the last lesson his father gave him — *when you see someone in a mirror who does not yet know his own face, will you know him to be yourself — or a stranger?*

"I am not Lin Wen," the figure said, stepping closer. "I am another keeper, from the chronicle. Perhaps what you have met, is the one of us three that you have never encountered."

Lin Wen's back went cold. He stepped back.

From behind the mirror came a sound — dried persimmon leaves scraping against something below, like a ginkgo leaf falling onto a quilt already asleep.

He felt something move in his mouth.

Moonlight lit the book he had brought back from the mirror days before and made it glow. A line appeared on the page:

*We are not watching a mirror. We are watching someone in a mirror read the page they have not yet read. And you — you are doing precisely what was done on our behalf.*

Lin Wen lifted the book. It was empty — he had finally understood his hand had been empty all along, and that this was exactly what he had always needed.

He held the book out to the one in the mirror and let it go.

The moon rose higher. The figure in the mirror finally caught it.

The moment the figure closed the book, the moonlight flashed once — every silver spot on the cobbled road flashed with it — and in their brilliance they spelled a new character:

*全。*

Lin Wen did not read it. He simply stood, feeling his heartbeat answer across the persimmon-shoots at the edge of the road — the same echo he had heard on the first night, over and over again. He knew this chapter had ended, but he had not answered the question yet:

*Who took the book?*

He thought about walking home — but home was far now. His first step back, however, landed precisely on the same flagstone where he had stepped on the first night of the chronicle.
