## One Hundred and Thirty-seven: The Light in the Empty Bowl

Thursday of the twentieth week after the spring equinox — morning — the sun had just come up.

A-Shi went to the stove-house first — his mother had not gotten up yet.

He crouched by the iron rim — the white bowl was still there — the fire burned steadily.

A-Shi reached out very close to it — he did not touch the bowl — he felt the heat between his palm and the porcelain.

He said nothing — his mother came in — she carried two bowls.

"Eat," she said — she set the bowls down — she did not look at the white one.

A-Shi did not answer either — he only glanced at it — the fire leapt once.

His mother saw — she set the bowls down — she went back into the yard — she didn't turn around.

A-Shi picked up his bowl — he looked at the white porcelain bowl — he felt it was whiter today than yesterday — and whiter than the day before.

The fire burned.

The fire burned.

The fire burned.
