## One Hundred and Thirty-two: The Second Night

Saturday of the sixteenth week after the spring equinox — afternoon — the sky so clear it hurt to look up again.

Aunt Guo sent the porridge up once more — the old keeper did not eat.

A-Shi sat before the stove again — he did not sleep.

Xiao Huo came from the side room — he pressed his small hand against the iron rim.

“Hot,” he said.

No one answered him.

That night — A-Shi fed the fire one birch — no more — no less — exactly a keeper’s measure.

He closed his eyes — he did not dare fall asleep — he only rested them for a while.

The fire stayed awake for him.

The next morning — the fire was still burning — A-Shi opened his eyes — he was still at the stove.

Everyone in the town understood by then — the old keeper wanted to hand the fire to A-Shi.

Aunt Guo brought the porridge again — A-Shi set it down for him — the fire burned on.

The fire burned.

The fire burned.
