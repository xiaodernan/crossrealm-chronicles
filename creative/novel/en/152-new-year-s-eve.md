## One Hundred and Fifty-two: New Year's Eve

Wednesday of the twenty-fifth week after the spring equinox — evening — the sky very cold — wind snuck in through the stove's seams.

His mother and A-Shi sat at the stove — the kitchen-god portrait was still pasted above — the paper edge curled a little — the fire burned.

His mother said: “Tonight is New Year's Eve — seven hundred years ago, the first keeper of the fire kept this night — he kept it until dawn — the fire did not go out.”

A-Shi nodded — he looked at the fire — the fire burned.

His mother said: “Seven hundred years — seven hundred New Year's Eves — the fire has not gone out for a single one.”

A-Shi did not speak — he counted the bricks of the stove — he counted once — he counted twice — he stopped.

The fire burned.

His mother said: “At twelve — the new year.”

A-Shi said: “The new year — does the fire have a new year too?”

His mother smiled — she did not speak — she looked at the fire.

The fire burned.

The sky darkened further — the wind grew stronger — the fire burned.

His mother moved her chair toward the stove by an inch — then another — A-Shi moved his too.

The fire burned.

It was almost twelve — his mother did not look at the clock — she touched A-Shi's hand — it was cold.

His mother said: “The new year is coming.”

A-Shi said: “The fire has not gone out — it has not.”

His mother nodded — she looked at him — she smiled — the fire burned.

Twelve — a firecracker sounded in the yard — on the distant mountain a patch lit up — then went dark.

His mother said: “The new year has come — and the fire is seven hundred and one.”

A-Shi looked at the fire — the fire burned — it burned — it burned.

His mother leaned against A-Shi — she said: “Keeping the watch is not about the fire — it is about the fire still being here — so long as the fire is here, the year is here.”

A-Shi did not speak — the fire burned — the fire burned — the fire burned.

The fire burned.
