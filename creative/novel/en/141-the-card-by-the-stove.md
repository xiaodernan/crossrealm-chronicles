## One Hundred and Forty-one: The Card by the Stove

Saturday of the twenty-third week after the spring equinox — afternoon — the sky very thin.

Mother set the white porcelain bowl back on the stove-top — she said nothing.

Aunt Guo came again — she carried a bag — one thing in it — she did not say what.

Aunt Guo set the bag down — she drew out a stack of paper cards — the fire did not move.

A-Shi did not ask — he kept the broom — he swept once — he swept again.

“Wash your hands,” Aunt Guo said.

A-Shi stopped — he dipped his hands in the basin — he looked up at Aunt Guo — Aunt Guo nodded.

Aunt Guo laid the cards out one by one on the table — the fire did not move.

His mother came back — she carried the rice — she saw them — she paused a step — she said nothing.

A-Shi saw — he kept sweeping — he swept for a long time.

Mother put the rice on the counter — Mother came back to the stove — she fed one more white-birch branch — the fire burned.

Mother said: “When did you learn those cards?”

Aunt Guo did not look up — “The old man gave them to me,” she said.

No one answered — the fire burned.

Aunt Guo taught A-Shi the cards — one by one — he studied each — he said nothing — the fire lit his hands — he finished — he said “Done.”

Mother brought the bowls — rice gruel — the four of them sat — no one spoke — the fire burned.

Aunt Guo said: “Don’t rush — I’ll teach you one a day.”

A-Shi nodded — the fire burned.

The fire burned.
