## Fifty-seven: The Embers

A month had passed since the bowl came alive again.

A-Shi still kept vigil. He fed the fire. He never asked anybody about it.

One afternoon his mother sent the boy for a pail, then fetched from the bottom of the wardrobe a small brown cloth bag, tied twice at the corners. She set it on the table.

Inside was a small ceramic firepot. Every corner had been blackened bright; every ridge had been worn smooth, thinned by generations.

"This was your grandfather's," she said.

A-Shi picked it up slowly.

Inside the pot were soft embers — still holding their shape, lightly settled into their own ash.

A-Shi did not ask.

He just held the pot in his hands and let the family history enter him.

His mother said:

"Your grandfather — at the end — gave this to me."

He nodded.

She looked at him a long time before she spoke again.

"He said — give it to you."

Then she added:

"He said — 'keep it; it keeps you.'"

A-Shi listened. He did not speak. His fingers tightened around the pot.

He set it back on the table. He sat silent.

The night he returned to the kitchen to cook, his mother stood at the doorway watching him.

She said:

"Your grandfather kept this same fire in his time."

A-Shi said nothing.

She nodded and left him to it.

The next morning, A-Shi sat beside the forge again. He stayed until dawn. He did not sleep.

By morning, the pot was glowing softly, still full of embers.

He said to himself:

"The fire has not died."

Then he closed his eyes and slept.

When he woke, the pot still burned.

A-Shi understood a great deal then — he understood he had simply heard what every generation had been told, but could never quite tell themselves.

The fire had never gone out.

It had only been waiting for him to accept it.

From that day onward — and every night after — the house never fell silent again. The pot held a shape steadily in the middle of the room, chest still warm in his hands. What he had been handed by his ancestors was the ember of every house that had ever stood against the cold.
