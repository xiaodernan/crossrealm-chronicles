## Sixty-four: The Beacon

A-Shi was fifteen.

On the night of the autumn festival, his mother took him up the mountain behind the house.

They climbed to the highest point on the western ridge, to a broad flat stone.

His mother lit a small oil lamp.

The light spread across that stone like a low tide reaching the shore.

From up there, A-Shi saw all along the eastern ridge a string of similar lights — small, orange, far away.

"Look," his mother said.

Each family along the mountainside had also lit their lamp for the festival.

Six hundred years of the same village doing this every year — generation after generation.

"Six hundred years?" A-Shi asked.

"Six hundred," she said.

A-Shi paid attention to the mountainside. Every light on it — they were all kindling fire.

His mother held her lamp up; he held his.

They stood together on the stone.

Some lights went out. More came on.

The mountain was alive with fire far into the valley.

"This is what your grandfather did," she said. "His father did before him."

A-Shi looked at her.

"Six hundred years?"

"Six hundred years," she said.

A-Shi's heart swelled with an enormous silence.

He realized — he is one of them. Fire follows through his family.

His father's grandfather and this grandfather stood exactly here, on this same ledge, with a small lantern in hand.

Six hundred years of hearth had burned, and every one of those people had been keeping it.

"Keep this light," his mother said.

A-Shi looked at his. His was the smallest. He could barely see it flicker.

He understood that this was exactly the job — a tiny light among others.

He would keep it. He would not stop.

From this night onward, every autumn festival, he would come up here.

Six hundred years of stars and light.

He would be the next.
