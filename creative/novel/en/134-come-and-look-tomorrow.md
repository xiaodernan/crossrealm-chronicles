## One Hundred and Thirty-four: Come and Look Tomorrow

Saturday of the eighteenth week after the spring equinox — morning — the weather clear.

Aunt Guo came as she said she would — early — the yard's stone seams showed fresh white again — the air clear as water.

She walked up to the iron rim — the white bowl was still there — the fire burning steadily. The morning's smoke slipped out of the stove-mouth in one thin thread.

She ladled out half a bowl of rice porridge — carried it over — set it to the left of the white bowl.

“The half pot of rice I cooked yesterday,” she said.

A-Shi did not look up — he kept feeding the white birch.

The fire burned.

Aunt Guo pressed the back of her hand against the iron rim — warm.

She watched for a long time. “The fire is well kept,” she said.

Xiao Huo sat up straight — he did not speak either.

Uncle Zhou was standing at the gate — standing further out than usual — he did not come in.

Aunt Guo picked up the white bowl — looked at it once — set it back down.

No one asked.

No one answered.

The fire burned.

Aunt Guo said one more thing when she left.

“I'll come and look tomorrow,” she said.

A-Shi still did not speak — the fire lit his hands — he put down the third birch branch — no more, no less.

The fire burned.

The fire burned.
