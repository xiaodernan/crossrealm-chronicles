## One Hundred and Sixty-four: Five Characters

Monday of the twenty-eighth week after the spring equinox — evening — the sky dark again.

His mother had put the box back where it belonged — but A-Shi remembered the five characters — “The seven-hundred-and-first year, up to here” — he lay in bed counting them — the ancient hand.

He got up — walked to the stove — the fire burned.

He said: “Ma — who wrote that character?”

His mother answered from the room: “The writer is long gone.”

A-Shi said: “Then why is it still glowing?”

His mother did not answer — the fire burned.

After a while, his mother came out — she sat before the stove — she stared at the fire — she said softly: “Because someone keeps adding wood for it.”

A-Shi said: “Who?”

His mother said: “You —”

A-Shi said: “Me?”

His mother nodded — she said: “For seven hundred years, every keeper has asked the same thing — every generation has had its answer — you are the seven-hundred-and-first year's answer.”

A-Shi did not speak — the fire burned.

The fire burned.

The fire burned.
