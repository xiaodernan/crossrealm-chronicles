## Fifty-three: The Vigil

In the evening, A-Shi set the clay bowl on the table. He spread the edge of the tablecloth under it to steady the base.

He went and fed the chickens, came back, and sat watching the bowl — untouched, and the candle stood straight.

Later — before bedtime — he woke all at once. The house was dark. The sky was still black.

He pulled the blanket slightly to see if the bowl was where he left it. It was — the flame still burned.

He watched a long time. Something glimmered very faintly along the rim. A pale ring, like morning leaking through something that remembered morning.

He did not move.

The bowl, he realized, was warm — warm against his palm from across the room.

He sat in silence.

Almost dawn, the sun crested the western ridge and the whole room filled with diffused light.

His mother came in. She said nothing.

She stopped at the doorway and saw A-Shi sitting with his head level with the bowl.

"You didn't sleep?" she asked.

"No," he said quietly.

"What did you see?"

"I didn't see anything," A-Shi said. "But it was watching me."

His mother nodded.

She came over, pulled up a chair, sat beside him. She spoke in a low, traditional way:

"Every night, when your grandfather lived, he sat this way — in silence — for a whole night at a time. He never slept. He did not say a word. He was teaching himself what could not be asked."

A-Shi didn't reply.

"That night," his mother said, "your grandfather would ask the bowl — why —"

She did not continue.

"— and no answer ever came — but those who sat heard it anyway."

The silence extended.

A whole day passed.

A-Shi waited for the lamp to go out.

It did not.

"A-Shi," his mother said, "go to sleep."

She reached for him and took his hand.

A-Shi dared not move. He closed his eyes.

He lay in bed — but he did not sleep. He thought about the bowl. It was teaching him, every second.

The next evening, he sat again.

His mother spoke to herself:

"That light — it has been burning for thirty years. This winter it will not go out."

A-Shi asked:

"How did Grandfather keep it?"

His mother considered before answering.

"— The bowl needs a person who sits and does not sleep," she said. And she stopped speaking.

A-Shi nodded.

He looked at the bowl and knew, very clearly:

— Grandfather had not left.

Grandfather had been here all along.
