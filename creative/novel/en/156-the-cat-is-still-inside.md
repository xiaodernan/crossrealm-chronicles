## One Hundred and Fifty-six: The Cat Is Still Inside

Monday of the twenty-sixth week after the spring equinox — morning — the sky just bright.

A-Shi pushed the door open — the wind and snow struck his face — he took a deep breath.

The yard was all white — his eyes went straight to the cloth laid at the door — it was entirely covered — the snow had covered it.

The cat slept on that cloth — seven hundred years — the keepers came and went — it only ever slept there — the fire burned.

A-Shi squatted down — he looked under the snow — under the snow there was a rounded lump — it let out a wisp of warmth — he laughed.

He laid both hands on the snow — he did not dig — it was inside — the fire burned.

He went back inside — he took two dry cloths — he pushed the door open — the snow was still falling — he spread the two dry cloths over the cat.

His mother stood at the door — she looked at A-Shi — she did not speak — the fire burned.

A-Shi said: “The cat is still inside.”

His mother nodded — she said: “Seven hundred years — it is just like that — when it is your turn, you will be just like that too.”

The fire burned.

A-Shi watched the snow fall — the fire burned — the fire burned — the fire burned.

The fire burned.
