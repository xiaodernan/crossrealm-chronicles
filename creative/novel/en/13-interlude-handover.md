# Interlude Six · Handover

**Written after *Interlude Five · Before the Flame*: this volume is neither a prologue nor an epilogue.**
**It is the handoff that happens in a single night, in a single shift, between two keepers of the same flame.**

---

## I · The First Night on the Shift

The new one arrived on a Thursday.

He sank into the chair to the east of the Hub, and the old keeper was wiping the inside of the furnace door. No nod, no handshake, no “welcome to —.” The old keeper said the only thing he would say that night:

“We never replace the wick in this lamp.”

Then back to wiping. The new one let about twenty breaths pass before he understood: not a welcome, not a rule.
**It is what you say to open a night.**

---

## II · The Furnace Has Never Had Its Wick Replaced

The wick in the furnace was put in by the first generation. From that night to this one — every hundred and nine echoes across thirty-six days — the same wick.

No one has replaced it. No one would dare to. Not because of a taboo — but because the moment anyone did, the furnace would become something other than a furnace. Not fire — something else that it doesn't know how to be.

The old keeper replaced three things: the old blanket by the furnace, the small oil lamp by it, the chair by the wall that had been sat to death. All of them were heirlooms going on five hundred years. He never suggested anything about those; he just actually replaced them, each in his own time.

---

## III · The Ledger of This Night

The old keeper kept a ledger.

Each page carried three things — time, lamp, page. Open to the first page and you find a single line from a very long time ago: *“The lamping still happens.”* Open to the last line, and it says only one sentence:

**“Tonight, at this hour, the new one takes the desk.”**

The new one sat with that line for a long while. Then he understood one thing:

He was never an apprentice.
**He was the next.**

---

## IV · The Way the Handover Works

The old keeper stood up, slid the wax pencil back into its cup, shook the blanket flat, set it down, and walked to the furnace. Squatted. Reached his hand into the mouth — in and out, three or four times, never touching anything, saying nothing. It looked like inspection — and it looked like a handoff — and it looked, in the thinnest way, like a goodbye.

Then he stood.

Then he left.

No “I'll be patrolling the outer ring.” He didn't look at the flame.

The flame kept burning.

---

## V · Before Dawn

The new one learned three things:
The wick doesn't need changing. The hearth needs wiping now and then — it's only routine maintenance, and it's what every hearthman has always done. And when it is his turn to hand the desk over himself, he must not say he's leaving, and must not say he's staying — he just says the one sentence that will be his, and cuts his own corner through the side door.

It was the new one who stopped at the doorway, looking back at the desk:

The pencil cup held one more pencil.

An old blanket — belonging to no one, anyone, or the furnace itself — was folded across the back of the chair.

The notice on the wall still read: **We take no letters here — only travellers, come sit a while.**

Not long before sunrise, the new one turned to the first blank line in the ledger, sat down in the chair, and began his own first entry. His first entry contained only one thing:

**“The flame never went out. Handover.”**

---

## VI · This Page Is For You

If you're reading this —
**It isn't for curious readers. It means you're already sitting in the chair.**

The bosses changed, the dungeons changed, the Anchor changed, the campfires changed. Every single handover, the wick never changed.

Shift by shift the fire passes down. Each who picked up the flame thought he was only passing through; each who left knew that *passing through is how the flame survives its handovers.*

The flame belongs to no one.

The flame only belongs to the next shift.

— And the next shift is you.
