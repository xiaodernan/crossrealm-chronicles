## One Hundred and Nineteen: Snow'an

On the fifteenth day after the spring equinox, Old Li came again — this time with a child.

Not his own.

It was Tiě mù sùmù, a boy who had been taken in by neighbors across the ridge. The child — eleven, or maybe twelve, with a gaze that went straight through anything he looked at — stood in the courtyard and did not move. He stood before the fire-seed tree for a long time. The tree's new leaves were unfurling in layers.

"I'm Snow'an," he said.

"Sit down," Mother said.

He did not sit. He kept looking at the stove.

"Your stove —" he said.

"炉火," Old Li said.

"炉火," Snow'an repeated.

He stared at the fire for a long time. Its light warmed his face to a soft gold.

Mother went to bring the rice out. A-Shi moved the low stool around the table so Snow'an could sit and eat with the family. Snow'an finally sat down. He did not speak.

"This house —" he started, very softly.

"This house is seven hundred years old," Mother said.

Snow'an considered it. "One thousand four hundred?"

"Seven hundred," A-Shi said.

"Two of you?" Snow'an asked.

"Seven," Mother said. Everyone laughed.

The fire-seed tree swayed.

"The tree is also seven hundred years old?" Snow'an asked.

"The tree is itself," Mother said.

"And the stove — seven hundred years old?" Snow'an asked.

No one answered.

This stove had ground flour for starving children in famine years. It had tended the dying during winters no one counted. It had received white birch roots from elders no longer living. This house never spoke of these things aloud.

炉火 burned.

Snow'an quietly reached his small hand toward the stove. The warmth came through the iron edge slowly. He pulled his hand back. The warmth stayed in his palm.

He stood up.

"I should go," he said.

"Come again tomorrow," Mother said.

He nodded.

"He wants to watch the fire," Old Li said.

He said that — and that was the only thing that got spoken for a long time in this house.

The fire-seed tree swayed.

Snow'an left without looking back.

The fire burned.

The yard was very old. Every grain of soil in it had been warmed by 炉火 for seven hundred years.

炉火.

Seven hundred years.

It was still here.
