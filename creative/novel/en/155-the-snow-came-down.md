## One Hundred and Fifty-five: The Snow Came Down

Monday of the twenty-sixth week after the spring equinox — morning — the sky not yet bright.

A-Shi woke — the yard was all white — the snow had fallen all night.

The fire burned — his mother was at the stove — she had not slept.

A-Shi asked: “Why did the snow wait so long to fall?”

His mother said: “It had its exam to finish in the house — now it will come out.”

A-Shi did not understand — she laughed — she did not explain — the fire burned.

His mother said: “Corn gruel — have some.”

She scooped a bowl for him — the gruel was still steaming — A-Shi held the bowl — he looked at the snow — the fire burned.

The fire burned.

A-Shi said: “I am going to sweep the snow.”

His mother said: “Finish eating first.”

A-Shi took a bite — he put it down — he looked at the snow again — the fire burned.

The fire burned.

His mother said: “Eat your fill — go out — the snow will cover the footprints — you have to finish the path before it does.”

The fire burned.

A-Shi put on his cotton jacket — he pushed the door open — the wind and snow struck his face — he took a deep breath — he went out.

The fire burned.

His mother watched the snow fall — the fire burned — the fire burned — the fire burned.

The fire burned.
