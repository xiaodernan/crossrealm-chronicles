## One Hundred and Thirty: The Stove Speaks

Saturday of the fifteenth week after the spring equinox — afternoon — the weather was beautiful beyond reason; the sky was a deep, clear blue like a fresh-washed white cloth.

Zhou came first. Then Aunt Guo.

The old keeper emerged from the back room.

He came and sat before the stove. The fire burned steadily. The keeper watched the fire for a long time. Snow was stuck to the sides of his head. The old keeper watched the fire — and said nothing. He put his hand into the fire-box — he let it hover a moment — he withdrew it.

"Stove," he said, voice low, gravelly.

No one answered.

"You have burned seven hundred years."

He didn't finish the next thought. Zhou didn't press. Aunt Guo didn't press. The fire burned. The fire in the box was a bright white — the old man put his hand back in.

"My whole life, I have been beside you."

The fire burned.

The fire burned seven hundred years.

The fire burned.

炉火 burned.

炉火 burned seven hundred years.
