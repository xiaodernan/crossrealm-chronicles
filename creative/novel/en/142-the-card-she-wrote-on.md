## One Hundred and Forty-two: The Card She Wrote On

Sunday of the twenty-third week after the spring equinox — morning — the sun had just come up.

Aunt Guo came a third day — she carried a small brush — and a blank card.

She said: “A-Shi — look.”

A-Shi turned his head — he saw what Aunt Guo held — she set the blank card on the table.

The fire burned.

Aunt Guo said: “Before the old man died — he said you would be able to read one.”

A-Shi did not ask — the fire burned.

Aunt Guo took a small brush — she dipped it in ink — she wrote one character on the card — she did not lift her head — she finished writing.

She handed the card to A-Shi — he took it — he looked at that one.

That one character — the one this house's fire could not say — A-Shi knew it — he said nothing.

A-Shi looked at it for a long time — he did not move — the fire burned.

His mother came — she saw the card on the table — she paused a step — she said nothing — she did not leave either.

Aunt Guo turned the card over — on the back was a date — “Seven hundred years, three months, twelve days” — Mother saw it — she said nothing.

A-Shi's palm was warm — he did not lower his head — the fire burned.

A-Shi said: “Done.” He set the card back.

His mother nodded — the fire burned.

Aunt Guo gathered the cards — she said: “From now on I'll teach you one every day.”

A-Shi nodded — his mother nodded too — the fire burned.

The fire burned.
