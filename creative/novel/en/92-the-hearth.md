# The Unchanted Chronicle of Crossrealm: The Keeper of the Anchor Fire

**Volume XIII**: New Fire — the Seven-Hundred Year

## Ninety-two: The Hearth

Late spring brought new leaves to every branch of the fire-seed tree.

Their needles were not the red of summer nor the amber of autumn — they were a deep clear gold, golden enough to look transparent against the sun. From across the yard, the tree glowed as though lit from within.

The stove had been burning for six hundred years, and would be six hundred and one more year when the seventh century of keeping was finished.

A-Shi sat at the stove every morning before sunrise. Xiao Huo sat beside him. His mother cleared the table behind them. None of the three of them spoke.

One morning his mother set aside the old bowl she had been using for years — the same bowl six hundred years of keepers had used — and walked to the stove. She reached her hand inside and withdrew one single thing: a lump of charcoal, no bigger than a nut, covered in grey-white ash so old it had gone almost soft to the touch.

She held it in her hand. It did not burn her.

Xiao Huo's eyes went wide.

A-Shi steadied his breath.

He lifted the coal from her open palm. Even this close, he could feel its heat without pain — as if the fire itself had grown soft from six hundred years of practice.

He saw, now: this was the same coal from the very beginning — the piece of kindling the first keeper had carried up the mountain, the piece that had burned — quieter and slower than any other — for six hundred years, waiting in the stove's deepest corner for the one who would finally recognize it.

The stove leapt once.

The yard's needles shivered.

A-Shi looked at his mother.

She placed the coal in Xiao Huo's hand.

The little boy held it, held it without being burned — and without flinching.

Six hundred years found their continuation in the hand of a child sitting before the same stove, the same fire, the same six-hundred-year gesture.

A-Shi closed his eyes.

He knew what the stove had been waiting for.

Xiao Huo placed the coal in the hearth, beside the embers, where six hundred years of keepers had placed their own.

The fire did not double in brightness. It did not flare dramatically. It settled — warm, even, steady, as though it had finally received what it had been waiting for all these years.

His mother turned once — glanced at A-Shi — then went back to her work.

The house filled with the scent of woodsmoke and pine resin.

Outside, the courtyard glistened in the spring light — the fire-seed tree's needles glowing golden against the blue sky.

A-Shi sat at the stove and felt the heat steady against his shins.

He knew, now, without any doubt — the story of this fire was not over. This house — this hearth — this six hundred years of fire will continue to burn. And the seventh century is just its beginning.
