## Sixty: The Firewood

One winter night, A-Shi sat beside the stove.

He was thirteen years old. The flame burned brightly.

His mother walked over carrying an old sword with a sandalwood handle — blackened at the pommel from contact with hands across six centuries. She placed it on the table.

"This is yours now," she said.

A-Shi looked up.

"Your grandfather's," she said.

He reached out to touch the blade. He realized it was heavier than the others he had known.

"It is the one he used to cut the winter wood."

A-Shi swallowed.

"This one," she said in English. "It followed him the whole journey."

"Six hundred years?" A-Shi whispered.

"Yes," she said.

A-Shi didn't know how to react. He put both hands around the handle. It held his fingers lightly.

"How many?"

"Six hundred years," she said again. "Every generation, every winter, after every night — wood comes. Six hundred years."

"Six."

"Yes."

"All fire?"

"All the fire."

He shut his eyes.

He imagined his grandfather on cold winter mornings cutting wood — his great-great-grandfather going into the mountain very early with his back against the same blade. Six centuries of it.

He felt a silence fall through the house. His mother and his grandfather had been here before him. They were all watching every moment his grandfather sat next to the stove.

"It will keep me," A-Shi said.

His mother did not blink.

"The handle," she said. "It remembers every hand that has ever touched it."

His mother told him: "Your grandfather used to say the family kept the hearth generation after generation. He never saw the end of it."

A-Shi asked one more question:

"Then what will I do with mine?"

"You," his mother said, "will keep it."

A-Shi heard her voice. A real sound, like the stove itself speaks.

He understood his role, the role his lineage carried for so long — he was not lonely. He would never be.

He would keep it.

Only some people know the fire truly belongs to everyone in the family.

A-Shi understood now.

He opened his eyes.

His mother was looking at him.

He nodded.

He took the sword.

"Then I'll keep it," he said.

His mother smiled.

No one needed to tell him anything else.
