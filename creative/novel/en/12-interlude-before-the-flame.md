# Interlude Five · Before the Flame

**Written after *Interlude Four · The Reply of the One Who Came After*: this volume is not the fifth letter — it is the paperwork from before any of the letters were inked. Fire wrote the first, the Mist wrote the second, Lin wrote the third, the One Who Came After wrote the fourth.**

**This is their shared rough draft — the hundred unattributed pages at the very bottom of the canon, the ones with no author and only footnotes.**

---

## I · The Nine Unlit Lamps

Nine unlit lamps, drifting.

Back then there was no *place* — only *before*. The Graywood was a lamp no sun ever reached; the Miller Wasteland was a lamp the sand had filled in on its own; the Shattered Timelines were a lamp whose rain had not yet agreed on a color. Every one of them was drifting in the same way — each had opened its mouth, and none of what it said had ever landed.

There was no mist yet. The mist would not learn its first syllable until the fire fell to the ground.

None of the nine had ever gone out.

Extinguishing requires a fire to exist — and fire was still asking itself whether it wanted to.

---

## II · Why Nobody Lit the Fire

Later someone wrote a fat book on this. The most important sentence in it was — **“Every realm assumed someone else was doing it.”**

The Miller Wasteland said the Hub had one; the Hub said the House of Hours had one; the House of Hours said the Deep Space jump had one; the Deep Space said the Graywood was waiting for someone else to move. The Graywood did not answer.

For untold ages no lamp opened its mouth — nothing spoken in that place carried; the unsaid sank into the ground, and stayed buried — until the fire fell.

Then someone stopped waiting.

He had been, in some way, an ancestor of Lin — not father, not father's father — he was **every** Lin's inner voice: the small voice that counts "how many steps left" every time Lin thinks about looking back into the fire.

He simply didn't wait.

---

## III · The Hundred and Eight Times He Nearly Left the Ember Behind

He was carrying a lump of coal on his back.

It was the one thing the fallen Dominion had owned that nobody had thought to confiscate. The Dominion did not fall to force — it was simply stood witness to, end to end, until it stopped.

Later, people turned that number into a tidy sum. It was no mere arithmetic — it was added together from two counts.

A hundred and eight — the travelers who had carried the same ember, set it down, and never picked it up again.

One — the one who never set it down.

He was that one.

He took the lump out for a look — and closed his hand around it harder.

**Fire does not need to be lit.**
**Fire only needs one person who refuses to let go.**

---

## IV · What the Fire Said When It Hit the Ground

The World Anchor fell here — at this very spot in the Crossway Hub.

Mark the word *fell* — not placed, *fell*. There was no expectation when the lump touched down: it was nearly spent, the sack was gone, thirty seconds of scavenged warmth remained, it was near midnight, the keeper-of-the-lamps' ancestor was not keeping any lamps, Lin's ancestor was not yet Lin, the mist did not exist, and fire did not yet admit it was fire.

It spoke one sentence as it fell.

It was not spoken by anyone alive, and it was not said by fire either — fire learned the line from "how many steps left":

**“One Realm to bind them all.”**

Nothing happened for ten seconds after. Then one more poster appeared on the wall. Then somewhere by a campfire, someone surfaced for a moment and said something unintelligible:

**“The flame never dies while the anchor holds.”**

— He had no idea he had just laid down a rule. He had simply woken up, once.

---

## V · The Former Life of the Mist, the Former Life of the Keeper

The mist is something that was born only after the fire fell.

It is the world's unsaid sentences. In the years before the fall, none of the nine lamps lacked for a voice — they lacked an echo. Whatever a lamp said, nothing would carry — and the unsaid, unheard, sunk down into the ground, with nowhere to go. After the fire landed, those buried words woke — and with no place left for them, they rose as one mist, drifting back over the nine lamps.

The mist *is* the shape of the act of opening your mouth, trapped. Every breath in is a question asked backward into the night; every breath out is an answer nobody held.

Which is why the mist **cannot be dispersed** — dispersion would only mean someone finally spoke. The mist wouldn't be gone; it would be speaking with a different accent.

The keeper was the first person to stop moving.

Not clever enough to investigate, nor foolish enough to charge in, nor far-sighted enough to plan — he just **sat down first**. Knees bent, shoulders against the direction of the beacon — he didn't sit down because he thought it was remarkable; he sat down because there was a little warmth and sitting was more reasonable than standing.

Every keeper since has simply been doing this: not clever, not foolish, not planning, sitting down, and watching the fire.

---

## VI · One Line, For the Next Reader

If you are cold while reading this, do not run toward the fire — the fire runs toward no one.

Put this at the very bottom of the canon:

**“By the time you reach this line, the hundred and nine echoes have possibly rung a few times.

No one keeps the tally, and none is needed.

I know you, and I do not, because I learned the syntax of fire: unsigned — therefore in need of no signature.

And you — you made it to this page.

Your turning it over decides exactly as much about the fire surviving as anything else ever has.

**The fire has always been waiting**.”**
