## One Hundred and Twenty-eight: The Card in the Stove

Saturday of the thirteenth week after the spring equinox — clear sky, that kind of light that stings your eyes a little. Uncle Zhou arrived first. Aunt Guo was second. Two strangers came from far outside town — no one knew them — they stood from the corner to the stove.

A-Shi was sweeping with a bamboo broom. Xiao Huo brought two bowls of broth and set them down.

Their grandfather came out from the back room and stood at the door. He didn't speak. He only watched the stove.

By six, a light rain began to fall. The town stopped leaving. No one went home.

The night was very long. The people by the stove decided that no one would leave before dawn. People were too tired to go anywhere anyway. A-Shi went to the kitchen and brought out dishes. Uncle Zhou and Aunt Guo insisted on staying the whole night. The grandfather stayed too.

No one asked about the cards. No one asked what the cards were for. No one asked where the cards came from.

They burned the cards, one by one, in the stove. The fire figured out what they were.

Seven hundred years, and these were the pieces of paper no one could bring themselves to speak of aloud.

炉火 burned.

炉火 burned.
