## One Hundred and Sixty: The Shadow

Monday of the twenty-sixth week after the spring equinox — evening — the dusk pressed the wall lower than by day.

A-Shi stood at the yard gate — he saw his own shadow — the shadow stretched very long — it reached outside the yard.

His mother was in the house — she heard he had stopped — she pushed the door open — she saw it too — the shadow.

He did not turn around — his mother did not speak either.

The shadow moved — it moved on its own — it crept slowly along the ground — it stretched out a hand — that hand was long — it was black — it was very old.

His mother said: “Come inside.”

A-Shi did not move — the fire burned.

The shadow stopped — it stopped in the middle of the yard — its hand rested upon the shadow of the stove — his mother saw it — his mother did not move either.

His mother said: “It has come back too.”

A-Shi looked at the shadow — the shadow looked at him — the fire burned.

The shadow was longer than before — it no longer moved — it just stood — it stood in silence — like a keeper of the watch — like every keeper of every year for seven hundred and one years.

His mother stood for a long time — she let out a breath — she said: “It is watching you.”

A-Shi nodded — he did not move either.

The shadow moved — it crept slowly back toward the yard wall — it vanished into the shadow of the wall — it could no longer be seen — the fire burned.

His mother said: “Go back inside.”

A-Shi went back inside — he went back to the stove — the fire burned.

His mother came in too — she pushed the door — she did not turn around — the fire burned.

The fire burned.
