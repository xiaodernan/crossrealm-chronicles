## One Hundred and Twenty-seven: The Waiting

Saturday of the twelfth week after the spring equinox.

The fog lay over the yard at dawn — not too thick — the sun was climbing.

Uncle Zhou arrived early.He did not speak.

The stove burned as it always had, watching.

Uncle Zhou stood in the yard for a long time — he wanted to leave — he didn't. He stood before the stove for close to an hour.

"You are here," he said to the fire.

The fire didn't answer.

A-Shi brought out a fresh bowl of warm rice gruel and offered it to Uncle Zhou. He took it, drank it in one gulp, and held the bowl.

"You've been carrying something," A-Shi said.

"No," Uncle Zhou said.

He stood there after finishing the bowl for some time.

Then he left.

The stove didn't go out.

The stove burned.

The stove burned.
