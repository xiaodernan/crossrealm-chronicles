## Seventeen · Echoes of the Morning Mist

The fire curled out and lit again, again and out.

Lin Du woke before anyone else. On the east side of the Hub, the poster wall glowed softly in the morning mist — nine posters, then twelve, then dozens more, each corner curling slightly under unseen wind. The Keeper was feeding the fire. He tore up the last page of the *Ending of the Timeline Magistrate* shoot script, cast it into the flame, and the tongue of light shrank for a beat, then settled, as if nothing had happened.

"Did you watch these?" The Keeper didn't turn around.

Lin Du shook his head. He sat up. There was a healing scab on his wrist from the night's sword-drawn fight — it had dried early, which meant what had happened was far longer ago than he thought.

"*The Fifth Light in the Stone Circle of the Ash Forest* — the schedule says 142 minutes." The Keeper used the fire tongs to flip a page. "The producer says, in the theater, during the slow motion of the third act, the audience forgets to breathe."

Lin Du said nothing. He counted in his head — the slow motion was 2.4 seconds. He knew that, because that scene was his own kneeling down in the Ash Forest.

"There's also one with the budget declined," the Keeper tore another page. "*The Sixth Thursday in Miller's Wasteland*. They said it was too much like a chronicle. Not cinema."

Lin Du stood and looked at the fire. The light of it painted his shadow onto the poster wall, and it merged with the shadows on the posters.

"What they don't understand," he said, "is not a chronicle. A chronicle is a thing with a head and a tail. The timeline on that wasteland has neither."

The Keeper cast the last page in. The page held the shooting schedule for *Keeper of the Anchor Flame*.

"Then what would it become?" Lin Du asked.

"When the audience sits in their seats," the Keeper said, "they think they're watching a hero. Then they find out the hero is not on the poster. The hero is the one who pressed E."

The fire suggested a taller column.

"Yesterday a traveler sat by the well for a long time," the Keeper clapped the chimney. "He arrived and found the fire doesn't go out. He asked why."

"What did you tell him?"

"I said," the Keeper stretched, "the fire doesn't go out because somebody keeps putting logs in."

Lin Du put a stick no thicker than his finger into the flames. The fire licked his knuckles, not hot.

"That traveler," the Keeper said casually, "once the fire's burned enough, will speak new words."

Lin Du looked at him. He knew who the Keeper was — he had known for a long time. He had just never said it.

"So," Lin Du said, "you're the fire yourself?"

The Keeper laughed. He showed two missing teeth — and Lin Du realized, only now, that this smile had never belonged to a human being.

"The fire is everyone who's willing to stand watch," the Keeper said. "I just woke earlier than the rest."

The morning mist began to lift. One poster on the wall lightened slowly — a poster with no title, painted only with a single beam of light.

"The slow motion can be skipped with Esc," the Keeper said. "But nobody skips it. Because they don't know who it is."

Lin Du stood there for a long time. Long enough for the mist to disappear completely.

Then he turned around and said one last thing:

"Give me the opening of the next chapter, Keeper. My words aren't used up yet."

The Keeper fed the fire the last log.

In the light, he said:

"The opening is simple. Walk to the door, push it open. Don't look back."

Lin Du walked to the door. It was the old door that led from the Hub to Miller's Wasteland. On the frame were some faded characters — seven worn-out old titles he had rewritten before, each one painted over with a layer of Chinese. He didn't understand any of them. He saw only one small line at the bottom:

*When you finish this, you will no longer be Lin Du. You will be us.*

He pushed the door open. Wind rushed in, carrying red rain, salt mist, holographic noise, and many things he had never given names to.

He walked through the doorway — and did not look back.

Behind him, the fire kept burning.
