## Eighteen · Sunscar Stair

The Sunscar Stair does not live on the star charts.

Lin Du turned through every atlas he could find, yet the names of the three suns were burned only into the deepest layer of his fingerprints, and every time he pressed E, the journal turned up only a blank page.

On the third day, he noticed a line etched behind the star map, paler than the stars themselves — written in a hand that did not match the rest of the atlas:

*The distance between suns admits no horn — only the drowned field of sky.*

He looked up and saw a third inscribed stone pillar. He remembered this one was the longest unnoticed. The three suns turned in their own right, one after the other crossing the tip of that pillar — as if cradling three fires not yet complete.

The sentence was carved into the pillar. Bare-handed.

The Keeper stood at the northwest corner, his back to him.

Lin Du opened his mouth, cold: "You lied to me. Why won't you say anything?"

"I cannot hear it," the Keeper answered. "The light comes from your hand."

The Keeper turned around. The fire in the bowl he held outshone the stars.

Lin Du shut his eyes.

He watched the three suns in a small shadow, swallowing and spitting them out one after another, eyes cold and drunk and bitter. The way the suns went down on their own made him shudder — every manner of sinking, seen completely, is also a manner of rebirth.

Hummm — the answer was in his heart. He was still on the second line:

*The one who answered never stood where the letter was dispatched.*

Lin Du opened his eyes.

He saw at the Keeper's feet a pile of ash. Colour was rising from the ash, as though three gates were stirring one after another. This was the first day.

The Keeper passed him a slip of paper.

There were only three lines:

**You can keep writing.**  
**The letters are not written by anyone in secret. They are waiting for you.**  
**The suns may go out — your story will not.**

Lin Du closed his fist — and this time he did not let go.
