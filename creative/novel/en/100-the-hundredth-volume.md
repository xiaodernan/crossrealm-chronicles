## One Hundred: The Hundredth Volume

The stove had burned for six hundred years. It had burned for seven hundred years after that.

A-Shi stood in the courtyard and lifted his eyes to the fire-seed tree. It stood very straight in the morning light, fresh needles glinting gold. His mother stood at the center of the courtyard. Xiao Huo was beside her.

She said the one word nobody had expected.

"One hundred," she said.

He nodded.

He did not speak.

He was remembering — every morning in this kitchen, every night on that stove, every leaf that had ever touched the flame, every keeper who had sat exactly where his mother now stood, smelling the same warmth that she was smelling now, knowing exactly what she knew.

The fire had never gone out.

Never once. Not for a single night. Not for a single hour. Not for a single breath.

His mother glanced at him for a long time. Something very old passed between them — six hundred years of keepers seeing each other across generations, each one saying without words what every other had said without words.

Xiao Huo tugged gently at the cuff of A-Shi's sleeve.

A-Shi placed his hand on the boy's head.

Xiao Huo's heartbeat.

A-Shi could feel it — small, rapid, new. A beginning.

His mother smiled.

"One hundred volumes, A-Shi," she said. "Write it."

He nodded once, slowly.

The wind passed through the courtyard. The stove flickered. The fire-seed tree bent gently with the wind.

One hundred volumes.

Six hundred years.

Seven hundred years.

They were both the same fire.

Xiao Huo counted the years in his fingers without quite knowing why.

His mother watched him do that. She smiled very slightly. She reached out and set one warm hand against the stove's iron edge.

The stove warmed her hand in return.

The fire warmed her.

Seven hundred years of warmth — seven hundred years of the stove's slow, patient, unbroken gift.

A-Shi placed his own palm against the iron edge of the stove.

The fire warmed him too.

The sixth-thousandth keeper would one day stand where he stood now, the seven-thousandth after that, the eight-thousandth, the nine-thousandth, the ten-thousandth.

Sixteen thousand years.

Fire after fire after fire.

A-Shi took a long breath.

Xiao Huo breathed with him.

The courtyard fire burned steadily in the morning sun.

The first leaf touched his hair.

The fire did not say a word.

It had never needed to.
