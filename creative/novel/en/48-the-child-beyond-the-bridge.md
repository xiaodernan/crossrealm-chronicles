## Forty-eight: The Child Beyond the Bridge

Five days after the spring equinox, the gate was knocked on.

A small figure stood in the doorway. A girl — no more than thirteen, skin deeply burnished by the sun, her shirt hem heavy with clay from the road. She was cradling a small terra-cotta vessel.

"The little one is here," she said.

Lin Wen came out of the house and stopped at the threshold.

"Your name is —"

"My grandfather is 墨鹮," the girl said.

His mother, standing at the kitchen doorway, held her breath.

"Your grandfather is called 墨鹮?" his mother asked.

The girl nodded.

"Your father — did he go while your grandfather —"

"No one goes before — they keep walking."

Lin Wen felt his breath catch.

The girl came closer. She set the vessel on the table and loosened the red cloth wound around its mouth. The cloth was layered three times, stitched with tiny, ancient-looking stitches. A smell of persimmon leaves and old oil rose from inside.

"My father was gone quickly," she said. "My grandfather said we have to carry a portion of the fire somewhere, and he could not carry it all the way."

Lin Wen knelt to look. He had seen the fire inside the vessel — a single spark, alive, flickering like a small animal. It was not going out. It had not been going out.

"This flame," the girl said, "is from the hearth of our house."

His mother could not speak. She lowered her eyes and pressed her lips together.

The boy A-Shi stood barefoot beside the table.

"Gran — 墨曦 will eat," Lin Wen whispered.

"I've come to deliver the fire," 墨曦 said, "thence I will return."

She sat down very carefully. She took one bite of each food his mother placed in front of her. When she finished, she looked at Lin Wen and his mother.

"Grandfather said that this is the third family on the route," she said. "The first house was the inn. The second was the mountain steward. This is the third."

"Then the fourth," Lin Wen said softly.

墨曦 nodded.

"He carries the fire to every final roof," she said. "My father could not finish it. My grandfather will not let it end."

She reached into her sleeve and drew out one small strip of bamboo on which characters had been inscribed in charcoal.

"My grandfather left this message."

Lin Wen took it.

**火。等。人。**

The stroke of 火 was half broken. The stroke of 等 was half written.

"He asks me to carry this to you," she said, "so that next season — the fourth house passes the fire on."

The whole room fell perfectly silent.

Their fire in the clay bowl stood steady — the flame was a steady line of straight gold.

Then it swayed once — once only.

They all looked toward it.

His mother finally spoke. She said one thing:

"Bring her a seat on the verandah. She has traveled far."

墨曦 lowered her face. She could not answer.

Later that night, after everyone had fallen asleep, Lin Wen woke to find the bowl fire extinguished. But over the ridge, beyond the hills and the old persimmon tree and the eastern slope — a very thin column of smoke still rose straight into the sky.

Far above them — his father's road was not finished.

墨鹮 was carrying the last light across the bridge — slowly, and completely — from one generation to the next.

But at least, tonight, the hearth in this room was lit.
