# Interlude Eight · Leaking Lamp

**Written after *Interlude Seven · Come Sit Awhile*: nothing in this volume is a letter — only a lamp that was never replaced.**
**The wax from inside the lamp will eventually finish leaking. The bowl in the bowl cabinet will always be there. This is how the station lets go.**

---

## I · The Leak

The wax that slipped from the lamp first fell onto the pedestal, later onto the table. It made no sound; it only pressed a small light into the grain, a slow seal. Each night the old keeper refilled the oil; somewhere down the line, he noticed that the thin white rim had already circled the base.

## II · Summer

The lamp burned through one whole summer. That summer there were many damp nights, paper-window panes that did not breathe, and so the flame lay against the wick for hours — as though someone had fallen asleep inside it. The wick grew short; the oil grew thin; only the wax kept coming down, a little more with each day.

## III · The Bowl

At the far end of the table, a bowl no one had ever used — its lip had a nick, turned toward the wall; in the cabinet sat other bowls behind it, and the ones who ate always reached for the nearest one first. In time, those nearest bowls were replaced many times; this one stayed clean, barely touched by dust.

## IV · The Cloth

One new keeper asked why the lamp was never replaced. The old keeper wiped the lamp-body with a cloth and said nothing. Wax stuck to it; it only loosened on the third pass. He folded the cloth and left it on the table's corner — as though it would come in handy for the next night.

## V · The Rainy Night

One rainy night, a traveller stayed a long time. After the meal, he set his own bowl on the table — its rim still faintly warm with the last of the heat; at the far end, the untouched bowl remained as it always had — not lifted, not turned, not moved. He walked out into the rain without his umbrella; the old keeper saw the traveller’s bowl on the table, carried it to the sink, and washed it clean.

## VI · Autumn

After summer, the lamp no longer burned as bright. The old keeper moved it against the wall; the wax ring on the table was fully exposed now, a thin circle — like a sentence no one had managed to finish, spoken a long time ago. Some nights the cabinet door did not quite close; the bowl lay at the far end of the table, rim turned toward the wall, pale in the half-light, almost too light to tell how long it had waited for someone.

## VII · The Replacement

Later came a new keeper; the wick was changed; the oil was refilled. The old wax was never scraped away, and the bowl was never taken out. In the ledger a single line was added: *table as before; one bowl at the far end, untouched.* No one asked who had written it; the wind through the window never touched the page.

## End

The flame crept low; the wax slid along the old trail, leaving a little more from the last time. The bowl still stood at the far end of the table, rim turned toward the wall, exactly where it had rested all along.

*(Interlude Eight · Leaking Lamp — the lamp will finally go dark. The bowl stays at the far end of the table, rim toward the wall. That is the way of the waystation.)*
