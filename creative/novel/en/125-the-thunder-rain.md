## One Hundred and Twenty-five: The Thunder Rain

The tenth Saturday after the spring equinox — afternoon came with a genuinely grey sky.

The whole town came. No one knew who had told them that today the stove would be burning loud. Everyone knew.

First Uncle Zhou arrived. Then old Aunt Guo. Then the man who had asked A-Shi to repair his stove yesterday — no one recognized him either. Then more than thirty people arrived. The courtyard was so packed that the door barely opened. The stove burned steadily. Nobody spoke.

His mother looked at it for a long time.

"Too many people," she said.

"Don't be afraid," A-Shi said.

Everyone took bowls for the meal. The pot was full of broth.

Rain came in earnest at three-thirty in the afternoon. The roof filled with the sound of water hitting tiles across the whole village. Inside the stove the coals popped softly under the damp.

The whole town sat watching the stove.

The ditch outside ran swift with brown water. The courtyard gathered rain before it could dry. Everyone in the yard sat quietly.

No one spoke.

When there were no visitors, the stove burned steadily.

When it rained, it also burned steadily.

Everyone waited that whole afternoon.

Then the rain stopped, and the sun came out.

The whole town shared one bowl of broth per person before anyone said a word.

The stove burned.
