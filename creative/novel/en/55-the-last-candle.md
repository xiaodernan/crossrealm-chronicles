## Fifty-five: The Last Candle

In the early spring night, a thin layer of frost settled on the yard.

Lin Wen beckoned A-Shi onto the porch. The moon lay across the old persimmon tree — its branches bare, nothing left but the trunk and the rope tied to the sapling.

"Come here," Lin Wen said.

A-Shi stepped closer.

"I have something for you," Lin Wen said.

From his coat he drew one small box — no more than the size of his palm. A brown wooden case with a single black scratch across the lid. No name. No other mark.

"Open it."

A-Shi opened it.

Inside sat a tiny slip of paper — in his grandfather's handwriting.

**阿石。用它点完。**

One single match lay beside the paper.

A-Shi did not speak. The match lay still, its head dark against the pale wood.

Lin Wen looked at him and did not speak either.

"This was his," Lin Wen said.

His voice was very low.

"His final night — he kept this next to his pillow."

A-Shi felt something he never had reason to question until this moment.

The match was small. It did not glow or speak. It only lay, as if waiting.

He folded the box inside his coat fabric, against his chest.

His mother, standing at the doorway, saw him come in and say nothing. She asked:

"A-Shi — what are you doing?"

He did not answer.

"That's the one your grandfather saved," Lin Wen said. "He knew you would watch the bowl this night."

His mother lowered her eyes. She turned back to the kitchen.

That evening, A-Shi sat alone beside the bowl.

The candle burned very low.

Soft light covered the table and the face of everyone sitting around it.

They ate without speaking.

After midnight, A-Shi dimmed and almost fell asleep again — then he caught himself.

He rose his head.

Out of the half-darkness, the figure at the doorway moved.

His grandfather stood there, in silence, without ever crossing the threshold.

He saw A-Shi.

He did not speak. He only looked at A-Shi — a steadying look.

A-Shi did not move.

His grandfather withdrew.

The second morning, A-Shi woke to find one single match wedged between the bowl and the side of the cupboard.

Propped beside his grandfather's former seat — never asked for, but always obvious.

Lin Wen looked at the match. He understood.

"He saw you tonight," Lin Wen said.

A-Shi nodded.

That was the moment everyone understood.

The fire had not died.

It had not gone anywhere.

The bowl had kept the light for all these years — and now the fire was clearly visible.

A-Shi looked at the bowl. The flame at its center trembled and then steadied itself, rising into a bright vertical sliver of gold.

The whole room filled with warmth.

His grandfather had not left.

He had only been further away than they knew.

The fire burned.

It was beautiful.

It was the whole family's fire.
