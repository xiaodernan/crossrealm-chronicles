## One Hundred and Fifty-one: The Little Year's Eve

Wednesday of the twenty-fifth week after the spring equinox — evening — the sky very cold.

His mother took out a bundle from the cabinet — she opened it — inside was an old print — a kitchen-god portrait — the paper edges yellowed — the picture showed a man squatting at the stove — smiling — the fire burned.

His mother said: “Today is the Little Year’s Eve — the stove offering.”

A-Shi glanced over — on the corner of the stove there was a small dish of sugar — he had prepared it in the morning — he did not know why he prepared it — he had.

His mother poured the white sugar into the dish — she poured a small bowl of clear water — she looked at A-Shi — he did not speak — he took the old lamp over — the wick burnt — the fire burned.

His mother said: “The stove offering is giving the fire something sweet — the fire does not eat — but hearts must be sweet.”

A-Shi nodded — he thought — keeping the watch is not about the fire — it is about the fire still being here — what is sweetness, then — he did not ask.

His mother said again: “Other people make the offering so the kitchen god can speak well of them up above — our family does not need to speak well — our family’s fire — seven hundred years — it does not need speaking.”

A-Shi looked at his mother — his mother looked at the fire too — the fire burned.

His mother pasted the portrait above the stove — she pressed the four corners — she smiled — she did not speak.

A-Shi said: “Then where does he go?”

His mother said: “Nowhere — he stays in the fire — he always has.”

The fire leapt — it burned — it burned — it burned.

The sky darkened — his mother put the sugar away — she put the water away — the portrait was still pasted there — the fire burned.

A-Shi looked at the kitchen god — the kitchen god looked at the fire — the fire burned.

The fire burned.
