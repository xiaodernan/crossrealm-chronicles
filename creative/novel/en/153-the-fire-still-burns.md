## One Hundred and Fifty-three: The Fire Still Burns

Thursday of the twenty-fifth week after the spring equinox — morning — the sky is bright.

The rooster crowed — his mother and A-Shi woke together — the fire burned.

A-Shi opened his eyes first — the first thing he looked at was the stove — the fire burned.

His mother opened her eyes too — the first thing she looked at was the stove — the fire burned.

The two of them looked at each other — neither spoke.

His mother said: “Seven hundred and one.”

A-Shi said: “Seven hundred and one.”

His mother got up — she added a little firewood — the fire burned brighter — it burned.

A-Shi brought yesterday's sugar dish — half a dish left — he looked at his mother — his mother looked at the fire — she did not speak.

His mother said: “Have some — no offering today — today is the fire's day.”

A-Shi tasted a little — very sweet — he did not speak.

The fire burned.

His mother said: “Seven hundred years — every single morning of every year — the fire was there — today is the same.”

The fire burned.

A-Shi looked at the kitchen god — the kitchen god looked at the fire — the fire burned.

His mother sat down — she poured a cup of water — she did not drink — she looked at the fire — she said: “The fire is still here.”

A-Shi said: “The fire is still here.”

The fire burned.

The fire burned.

The fire burned.
