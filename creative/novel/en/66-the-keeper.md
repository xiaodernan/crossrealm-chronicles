## Sixty-six: The Keeper

A-Shi was eighteen years old.

His mother came to him one morning at the kitchen stove. She had in her hands one folded letter.

She extended her hand.

"Read this."

He read it. The line was simple and insistent.

Six hundred years we have kept the fire. To you henceforth.

A-Shi looked at the stove.

The fire was bright and stable. No one had ever let it go out for six centuries.

He asked: who kept it?

His mother answered without emotion.

"Grandfather's grandfather's grandfather's grandfather. Everyone before you."

Six hundred years. Every generation. Every hearth. Every minute.

He heard it now.

A-Shi felt the full weight of the centuries that had been handed to him.

His hand stretched to the stove. He added wood.

He understood now what they meant when they said the stove had never died.

Six hundred years.

Six hundred nights.

Every generation.

No one had ended it.

A-Shi finally understood something very deep about this house.

He had grown up inside the fire's life.

He simply had not known how to see it.


A-Shi laid both palms on the stove.

The warmth spread through them.

Six hundred years of warm, undying fire.

He was its next child.

Six hundred years of hearth.

Every one of them trusting whoever would come next —

the next to hold the logs, to add the sticks, to keep the blaze.

Six hundred years.

Six hundred years.

Now him.
