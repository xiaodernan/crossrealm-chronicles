## Sixty-two: The Last Stick

Spring was leaving behind its last warmth.

A-Shi walked up the mountain every day.

He reached the oldest persimmon tree on the slope.

Its trunk was as wide as a hut. The top branches vanished into smoke against the sky.

A-Shi remembered his grandfather standing below it.

He planted his feet.

He raised the sword and prepared to cut one of the dry branches.

He stopped.

He decided not to cut.

He squatted down to the dry persimmon leaves around its roots.

He thought about his grandfather — then about his own father, and then his father's grandfather.

Each generation had sat under this tree.

They had all kept the fire alive.

He lowered the sword.

Slowly he said to the tree:

"I understand."

He knew now — this family is a keeper family, and he was its next fire-keeper.

He picked up a few dry branches the family had already shed.

He turned and went down.

He built the stove with them.

He lit it carefully.

The fire grew remarkably. The whole kitchen shone.

His mother opened the door behind him and smiled.

"You understand," she said.

A-Shi nodded.

He went out to the yard and sat in the moonlight.

The moon had come up higher, not yet a full disc.

He closed his eyes and held his sword to his chest.

The wind came over the ridge. A-Shi found that his body felt vast as a sea that no one crossed, but the path had been there all along — he was the keeper now, carrying what his grandfather had carried.

He would be previous-generation's next grandfather's grandfather.

He held more for the fire than for anything else.
