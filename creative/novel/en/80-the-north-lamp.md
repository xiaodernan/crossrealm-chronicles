# The Unchanted Chronicle of Crossrealm: The Keeper of the Anchor Fire

**Genre**: Planar fantasy / dark epic / metafiction
**Adapted from**: Crossrealm Chronicles
**Volume XII**: The Long Journey — Fire and Ink

## Eighty: The Lamp of the North

On the third day after leaving home, A-Shi crossed the chimney ridge.

Beyond the ridge lay a vast black flatland nothing he had ever seen — wide and dim — and in the far distance, a row of tiny yellow-orange lights hung above the horizon.

He walked toward the nearest lamp.

An old man was already sitting before it — roughly his mother's age, with an identical iron canister on the ground at his feet.

The old man looked up at A-Shi.

"Are you the six hundred and first?" he asked.

A-Shi nodded slowly.

The old man smiled.

"Six hundred and one," he said. "Three lamps across the northern flatland. I tend the first."

A-Shi stood still.

"Your family —"

The old man nodded.

"North lamp: my house. Middle lamp: my brother's. Yours — south — is the third. Six hundred years, each family tending one, all three lighting the ridge in perfect silence, never touching but never going out."

The old man said:

"I have been looking for you."

A-Shi froze.

"For six hundred years?"

The old man shook his head.

"Until now."

The old man dipped a small ladle into his own canister — it was filled with the same ash-slurry A-Shi had kept at home — he poured a single spoonful into the lamp's basin. The lamp was very small, very steady, blue and silent.

He looked at A-Shi and said:

"Your fire — I have smelled it for years."

A-Shi asked:

"How could you know?"

The old man said:

"The wind. The north wind carries whatever is burning on its path. We have been smelling your fire for centuries — the other two lamps — and we have always known which direction it comes from."

He did not elaborate. He touched the ladle to the lamp. The tiny lamp-head flickered — low and steady, blue and ancient.

And then — across the horizon — A-Shi saw all three lamps glow at once, connected by an invisible line of warmth that swept across the flatland.

The old man handed him a smooth wooden plaque. Someone had carved it long ago, in tiny characters:

守火 — 守到全案。

Translation: Tend the fire — until it all comes home.

A-Shi asked:

"What does that mean?"

The old man answered:

"'Keep the fire until it has spread to all of them.' Your great-great-grandmother's sister told me that line when I was a child. She was never interested in the word for 'everywhere' that the world uses now. She had one word of her own — quan'an, the whole case, the entire matter. She meant: keep the fire until all of them are alight."

A-Shi accepted the plaque with both hands.

The old man watched him carefully.

"What day is it today, in your calendar?" he asked.

A-Shi considered. He did not know.

The old man smiled faintly.

"Six hundred years and one day," he said. "Your stove has been my lighthouse — I have watched it every night, every night of my life. I know you don't lack for anything. The fire keeps waiting."

The old man stood.

"Two days north from here, you will find the second lamp. Keep walking east."

He pressed a packet of kindling wood, a smaller iron canister of white ash, and a single copper-rimmed bowl into A-Shi's hands.

A-Shi accepted them.

As the old man reached to lower the lamp, he paused, cast one look eastward — at the distant red blush A-Shi could now see trembling on the far horizon, at the first breath of the sun's light — and said softly, almost to himself:

"For six hundred years, this ridge has never once been fully lit."

A-Shi looked at the three lamps beyond.

He nodded.

"Tonight," he said.

The old man smiled.

He let the lamp rest in its basin.

"Then walk on," he said.

A-Shi turned east and walked into the darkening sky, toward the second lamp.

He had only just set out — but somewhere in the world, in kitchen after kitchen, in the faint warmth of a hundred stoves, someone was already lighting their lamp, as if answering.

The horizon was coming alive.
