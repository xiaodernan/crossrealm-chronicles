## One Hundred and Twenty-one: The Fire Comes

Week six after the spring equinox — a Saturday before daylight.

Mother had already risen. The dumpling filling was mixed — cabbage and pork.

Xiao Huo sat in front of the stove, silent, watching the fire.

Snow'an should have arrived by now.

He came — with company.

His mother trailed behind him.

A-Shi stood up from the straw mat and stepped closer.

"Mother-of-Snow'an," he said.

Snow'an's mother stopped in the doorway. She looked at the fire.

The stove burned steady — through the doorway they could see it faintly glowing.

"I'll sit here," she said.

She sat down beside the stove.

She didn't speak.

The entire household came inside.

Mother brought the dumpling filling over.

A-Shi said, "Making dumplings."

Mother and A-Shi sat down to make dumplings.

Snow'an's mother stayed in front of the stove, watching the fire.

She did not speak.

The stove burned.

The fire burned.

Sometimes a family does not speak — they simply sit together — the fire leaps up from within the stove — every person is silent — silence is the deepest language.

Snow'an's mother sat like that for almost two hours before she spoke.

"From now on," she said.

She stopped.

"From now on, I will come every Saturday," she said. "I'll come to watch the fire."

No one stopped their hands.

The dumplings were finished.

Mother served one bowl to each person.

The whole family sat down in the kitchen and ate dumplings.

Snow'an's mother held the bowl.

She ate.

This house was seven hundred years old — no outsider had ever sat in this kitchen before.

Everyone in the family knew.

Half a pot of dumplings remained.

The fire-seed tree swayed.

Within days — the whole village learned — Bai Yin's stove — the one across the ridge — now had a guest.

And from that day forward — every Saturday — Snow'an's mother came.

And watched the fire.

This house — seven hundred years old — had finally gained one.

炉火.
