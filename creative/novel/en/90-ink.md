# The Unchanted Chronicle of Crossrealm: The Keeper of the Anchor Fire

**Genre**: Planar fantasy / dark epic / metafiction
**Adapted from**: Crossrealm Chronicles
**Volume XIII**: New Fire — the Seven-Hundred Year

## Ninety: Ink

On the winter solstice all the stove's flames had been bright for three months.

Snow was falling heavily that night. A-Shi's mother was back from the mountain carrying wood, resting by the stove. She watched Xiao Huo standing before the ledger's last page.

He was holding the brush.

A-Shi had not set the ink already. His mother was watching too. The stove ticked once.

Xiao Huo dipped the brush in the ink, raised it to the last empty page of the ledger, and wrote.

What he wrote was not a character he had ever seen.

He wrote:

Fire.

Not the ordinary word for it. Not any character. It was something the fire had transmitted through his hand — a single stroke of pure combustion, incised into the paper, glowing faintly red as the ink spread.

The moment the stroke touched the page, all six hundred years of fire in the room leapt at once.

His mother pressed both hands to her chest.

Outside, the fire-seed tree shook once — every needle on every branch trembling together.

Far away, over the cloud-heavy ridge, no light shone and no dark lay.

The stove flared so high the entire kitchen turned white.

A-Shi saw — in that instant, in the heart of the flame — a silhouette standing. Not clearly. Not completely. An old, old figure — ancient beyond measure, the feet planted into the earth, the silhouette the shape of someone made from the fire itself — she looked once at Xiao Huo, once at A-Shi, once at the whole kitchen — and vanished.

The stove flickered.

All through the night, deep midnight, the fire burned steady and silent.

A tear slipped down Xiao Huo's cheek.

A-Shi sat down beside him, wiped it away gently.

"Did you see who?" he asked.

Xiao Huo wiped his own eyes.

He was quiet a long while.

Then he said:

"I saw all six hundred years of them."

A-Shi did not say anything.

Xiao Huo said softly:

"They are all here."

He paused.

"I could smell their hands."

He set the brush down.

He stood, slowly, cradling the ledger against his chest.

Outside, the snow fell deeper over the yard, over the fire-seed tree, over the house and the stove, over the two generations of keepers keeping the same watch — one generation closing, one generation opened, all of it burning, all of it one breath of the same fire.

The stove kept the fire as the stove always had.

It would keep it still.
