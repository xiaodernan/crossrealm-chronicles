## One Hundred and Twenty-nine: The Thin Rain

Saturday of the fourteenth week after the spring equinox — midday — a very fine rain started to fall — so fine it barely fell, just hovered in the air — the whole town came.

All the people of the town crowded into the kitchen. The rain turned the courtyard into a wet mirror and nobody noticed. The stove burned. Zhou arrived first, then Aunt Guo, then all the people, everyone came inside — those who came were already so soaked it hardly mattered.

A-Shi brought in hot broth and thin porridge. The people sat down slowly — each person's face turned a little warmer when the fire licked the iron.

Nobody left. The whole afternoon, until the sky went dark. The stove never went out. The people of the town remained until the hour of the fifteenth when the emptied bowls were gathered and carried to the back room — finally they left, one by one, carrying something home that had never been there before.

The stove burned.

炉火.
