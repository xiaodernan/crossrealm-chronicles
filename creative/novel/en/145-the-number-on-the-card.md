## One Hundred and Forty-five: The Number on the Card

Sunday of the twenty-fifth week after the spring equinox — afternoon — the light crisp and bright.

Aunt Guo laid the cards out one by one on the stove — a stack of seven — she counted to the top.

A-Shi watched — he did not speak — his mother sat at the stove too — the fire burned.

Aunt Guo turned one over — she held the character up to A-Shi —

“Remembered it?” she said.

A-Shi nodded — his lips moved — he did not speak — the fire burned.

Aunt Guo folded that one — she took it back — she drew another out — the same character — she handed this one to A-Shi.

He looked for a long time — he folded it once — he unfolded it — he looked again.

His mother said: “Your sleeve is stained.”

A-Shi did not move.

His mother did not speak — the fire burned.

A-Shi suddenly smiled — it was a small smile — Aunt Guo watched him — she did not answer — the fire burned.

Aunt Guo said: “Remembered. The seventh card.”

A-Shi put the cards away — he set them back on the stove — he looked at his mother — she was smiling too.

The fire burned.

The fire burned.

Aunt Guo said: “We’ll talk tomorrow.”

A-Shi nodded — the fire burned.

His mother nodded — Aunt Guo nodded too — the fire burned.

The fire burned.
