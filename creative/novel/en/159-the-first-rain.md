## One Hundred and Fifty-nine: The First Rain

Monday of the twenty-sixth week after the spring equinox — afternoon — the sky darkened for a moment — the rain came.

A-Shi stood in the yard — he stretched out a hand — a few drops caught on his fingertips — very light — still carrying a bit of winter's residual cold.

His mother was not at the stove — she was in the house — she heard the rain — she pushed the door open — she looked at A-Shi.

She said: “Come inside.”

A-Shi said: “Just for a moment.”

The rain came down without urgency — one drop at a time — striking the blue-grey stone slabs in the yard — the sound of ceramic.

His mother stood at the door — she did not insist — she just stood — waiting.

The fire burned.

The rain grew a little heavier.

His mother said: “Seven hundred years — this is the first rain of the seven-hundred-and-first year — last year it came crying — this year it comes smiling.”

A-Shi tilted his face upward — rain fell on his face — he did not move.

His mother said: “Last year, after your grandfather left, the rain came — that one — came crying.”

The fire burned.

A-Shi said nothing — he stood for a while — he went back inside — he did not wipe his face — his mother did not hand him a towel — the two of them sat at the stove like that.

The fire burned.

The fire burned.
