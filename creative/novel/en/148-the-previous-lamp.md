## One Hundred and Forty-eight: The Previous Lamp

Tuesday of the twenty-fifth week after the spring equinox — morning — the sky just bright.

A-Shi woke — the fire still burned — his mother was already sitting at the stove — she did not look at him.

In the corner of the stove there was a lamp — an old lamp — a stone base — no shade — the wick burnt — A-Shi had never seen it.

He walked over — his mother did not stop him — she only looked at the fire.

A-Shi picked up the old lamp — it was very light — there was no oil in it — on the stone base there was a line of characters — he could not make them out — he held the lamp close to the stove — the firelight fell on the line — the characters were deep — they were carved long ago.

His mother said: “Your mother’s father kept the night-watch with this.”

A-Shi did not speak — he looked at the line — he slowly made it out — it read: “Light it once — burn for seven hundred years.”

He lifted his head to his mother — his mother still looked at the fire — she did not look up.

His mother said: “Before he left, he set it at the stove — he said — the next keeper of the watch — is you.”

A-Shi did not move — the fire burned.

His mother said: “It is not a lamp — it is a thing — it remembers seven hundred years — it remembers your great-grandfather — it remembers your grandfather — it remembers your father — it remembers you — it will keep remembering.”

A-Shi set the old lamp back — he looked at it again — the wick burnt — there was no oil.

His mother said: “No oil — the fire itself is its oil — so long as the fire burns, it is.”

A-Shi looked at the fire — the fire burned.

His mother stood up — she came over and laid her hand on his head — she said nothing — she just kept it there.

The fire burned.

The fire burned.
