## Twenty-two: After the Mist Scatters

Lin Wen sat for a long time under the persimmon tree by the door.

The mist cleared more slowly than he had arrived. It was the first time, he realised, that he had ever walked through a door that he himself had truly been shown to — not one granted by others nor won in an adventure, but a door that no one had ever needed to nail shut, where he could say, plain and simple, *remember this.*

He met his younger self.

Not to contend or to reconcile. They only exchanged one question.

"Did you find the fire?" the younger Lin Wen asked.

Lin Wen did not answer right away. He looked at his hands — which held a few pieces of dry kindling he had picked up along the mountain road. That was not a flame; but it was something someone else might use to keep a flame alive.

"I did not find it," Lin Wen said. "I found the people who would keep it."

The younger self nodded and returned to the house's other worlds. The door settled back to being a door. Lin Wen did not move; he could hear the villagers fixing the stone path his father had begun before he died. No one asked him what he had discovered; someone simply came to his shirt pocket and tucked a match into it, without a word.

Before he walked out again, he looked at the row of rooftops one last time. Every tile of them held a name he had once read aloud. The pomegranate tree at the village gate had grown taller.

He did not know where he would go — perhaps not far from home at all, perhaps nowhere, perhaps only inside this one room he had finally chosen. It could be that at that moment, a light had already fallen on the words in front of you now.

That was the road.

If you want to ask the same question, I left the match by the door — it is yours.
