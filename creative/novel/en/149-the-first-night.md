## One Hundred and Forty-nine: The First Night

Tuesday of the twenty-fifth week after the spring equinox — evening — the sky fully dark.

His mother finished supper — she stood up — she untied her apron — she laid it on the table.

She said to A-Shi: “Tonight — you keep the watch alone.”

A-Shi lifted his head — he did not speak — he looked at the fire — the fire burned.

His mother walked to the door — she stopped — she did not turn around — “If the fire goes out, you go too.” she said.

She went out — the yard was very quiet — the gate closed — the latch sounded once.

A-Shi stood alone at the stove — the fire burned.

He sat down — he watched the fire — he counted how many times it jumped — he counted seven — he stopped.

He thought of his mother’s words — “Light it once — burn for seven hundred years” — he thought of the line on the old lamp.

He took the old lamp over — he set it by the stove — the wick burnt — there was no oil — the fire burned.

A-Shi said: “I’m here.”

The fire leapt once — it did not go out — it burned.

The night deepened — he did not sleep — he just sat — he watched the fire — the fire burned.

He counted the fire — one — two — three — he counted seven — he started over — one — two — three.

The sky was almost bright — the fire still burned.

His mother came back — she stood at the door — she looked at A-Shi — she said nothing — the fire burned.

His mother said: “It did not go out.”

A-Shi said: “It did not go out.”

His mother came over — she sat down — she watched the fire with him — the fire burned.

The fire burned.

The fire burned.
