## One Hundred and Thirty-five: The Third Morning's Breakfast

Tuesday of the nineteenth week after the spring equinox — morning — the sun had not climbed yet.

Uncle Zhou arrived again — he was carrying a small iron pot from home — half full of dry rice.

Aunt Guo was there too — she was sitting at the stove — four bowls on the table: A-Shi's, Mother's, Xiao Huo's — and one white porcelain bowl, still empty.

A-Shi kept feeding the birch — no more, no less — exactly the keeper's measure.

Mother brought the porridge out — she set two bowls down — A-Shi took them over — set them at the stove — he did not look at the white bowl.

Xiao Huo was at the table too — very quiet.

Mother glanced at the fire. “It kept the night,” she said — a very short line.

Aunt Guo did not look up either. “The fire keeps itself,” she said.

Mother looked at her a second time. “You keep it too,” she said.

Uncle Zhou set his bowl down beside the empty white one — he did not eat — he only set the dry rice at the stove's edge.

Mother picked up the little iron pot — she studied it for a long time — she did not ask whose it was — she set it on the table.

The fire burned.

Mother went back to the kitchen — she brought out one bowl of plain porridge — set it beside Uncle Zhou's pot. “This is yours,” she said.

No one spoke again — A-Shi put the third birch branch into the stove.

The fire burned.

The fire burned.
