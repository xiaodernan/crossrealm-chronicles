## One Hundred and Sixty-three: The First Light

Monday of the twenty-eighth week after the spring equinox — morning — the sky just brightening.

His mother brought out the old box from the stove — it was very old — its edges cracked — she lifted the lid — inside lay a book — a leather cover — deep brown — a copper sun-disk nailed to the front, glowing faintly.

She said: “This is the first page of the book.”

A-Shi leaned in — the fire burned — he could not quite see.

His mother said: “It doesn’t show itself to just anyone — today you may see a glimpse — do not read any further.”

A-Shi nodded — he reached out — his mother said again: “Slowly.”

A-Shi paused — he slowly extended his finger — gently turned to the first page.

The fire burned.

On the page were only five characters — “The seven-hundred-and-first year, up to here” — in an ancient hand.

A-Shi did not speak — the fire burned.

His mother closed the box — she did not let him look any longer — she put the box back where it belonged — she did not speak — the fire burned.

The fire burned.

The fire burned.
