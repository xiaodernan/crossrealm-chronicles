## One Hundred and Twenty-six: The Shadow at the Stove's Mouth

Saturday of the eleventh week after the spring equinox — the sun had barely risen — the yard was still cool and clear.

A-Shi was sweeping with a bamboo broom while Xiao Huo watched him from the porch.

"Eggs today," Mother called from the kitchen.

A-Shi set the broom aside and gave a small nod. The sky was very wide, surprisingly blue. The fire-seed tree's needles had grown another layer — their serrated edges shimmered in the morning light.

He turned back to the kitchen and saw Xiao Huo standing at the stove with his hand flat against the iron edge. He had not moved.

"Did you hear it?" Xiao Huo asked.

A-Shi stopped for a moment. "Hear what?"

"The stove," Xiao Huo said. "Listening to it."

A-Shi crouched down and listened.

The stove was making a sound — a low, steady sound — not loud exactly, but unmistakably there. A-Shi put both hands on the iron edge.

The iron was warm.

"灶火," he said. "炉火."

Xiao Huo nodded slowly.

He reached out and pressed his small hand against the iron.

"Seven hundred years," he said quietly.

Nobody answered.

The stove burned.

The stove burned.
