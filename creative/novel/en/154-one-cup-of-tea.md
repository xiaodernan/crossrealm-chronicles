## One Hundred and Fifty-four: One Cup of Tea

Friday of the twenty-fifth week after the spring equinox — morning — the sky still wet with dew.

His mother boiled the water — she took out two bowls — one for her — one for A-Shi.

A-Shi looked at the two bowls — he asked: “Two?”

His mother said: “Two keepers of the night — two bowls.”

She poured the tea into the bowls — the first for A-Shi — the second for herself — she pushed one bowl across the table to him — the fire burned.

A-Shi held the bowl — he smelled it — a faint bitterness — he took a sip — it was very hot.

His mother said: “Small sips — don't scald yourself — and don't scald the fire either.”

A-Shi laughed — she laughed too — the fire burned.

His mother said: “The tea was the same in your grandfather's time — a bowl for each — the same in your father's time — me — A-Shi.”

The fire burned.

His mother looked at A-Shi — she said: “You drink very slowly — as if waiting for something.”

A-Shi said: “I am not.”

His mother nodded — she did not ask again — she too drank slowly.

The fire burned.

The two of them finished — the bowls were empty — the fire burned.

His mother washed the bowls — she set them on the table — she did not look at A-Shi — the fire burned.

The fire burned.

The fire burned.
