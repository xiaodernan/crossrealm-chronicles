## One Hundred and Forty: Seven Days of Silent Fire

Saturday of the twenty-second week after the spring equinox — afternoon — the sky very flat.

This week — Aunt Guo did not come.

The fire burned all the same — Mother fed the birch all the same — A-Shi kept watch all the same — they did not speak of it either.

Mother moved the white porcelain bowl from the stove to the shelf — she wiped down the stove — she swept it once — she said nothing.

Uncle Zhou came the next day again — he stood at the stove — the fire burned — he did not come in.

Mother asked him: “What are you waiting for?”

He shook his head — “Nothing.” he said.

Mother nodded — “Sit down and have tea,” she said.

He did not sit — he said: “I’ll just look at the chickens.”

He left — he said it very softly.

The fire burned.

On the third day — morning, the sky not yet light — A-Shi woke — he did not eat — he went straight to the stove-house — he looked into the firebox — it burned very evenly.

He glanced at the shelf — the white bowl sat at the top — the fire lit it — it was very white — whiter than the day before — and whiter than the day before that.

He reached out very close to it — he did not touch it — he felt the heat against the back of his hand.

He said nothing — his mother woke — she carried two bowls of porridge — she said nothing — she set them on the table — she did not touch the white bowl either.

A-Shi picked up his bowl — he looked at the fire — he did not speak.

The fire burned.

The fire burned.
