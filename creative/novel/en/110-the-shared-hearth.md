## One Hundred and Ten: The Shared Hearth

The night before the autumn equinox, a light rain fell on the courtyard.

By dawn it was gone. The ridge of the white stone was already glowing.

His mother rose before the light.

She took out every bowl and plate the house owned — six bowls, four plates, three pairs of chopsticks — and set them on the table, one by one.

"Too many," she said.

"Not enough," A-Shi said.

This house had not seen this many bowls on the table since the day A-Shi was born.

Xiao Huo had changed into a clean set of clothes. Beneath the outer layer, he was wearing an old hemp robe — the same old hemp robe that had once belonged to Bai Yin, that his grandmother had tucked away in the iron box six hundred years before. His mother had remade it; it hung down to his ankles.

Xiao Huo looked down at his bare feet.

His mother smiled and said nothing.

The gate to the courtyard rattled once.

Softer than the last time — not a push, just the gate noticing him. A man walked in from the road.

It was the old man.

He carried a pack on his back today. Lower than before — he was shorter than before. He had changed again. He wore a hemp robe even whiter than the last, even thinner, the cuffs re-edged recently so they would not fray.

A-Shi's mother knelt in the courtyard.

A-Shi knelt beside her.

The old man knelt too.

Three keepers, kneeling in the courtyard, saying nothing.

The wind moved through the fire-seed tree. Every needle bent west — the whole tree turned toward the stove.

Xiao Huo walked out from beside the stove.

He did not run — he walked. He held a porcelain jar in both hands — the jar filled with ash from their own stove.

He lifted it to offer to the old man.

The old man accepted, then reached into his pack and produced a smaller jar — the ancestor-ash from the ridge. He offered it to Xiao Huo.

The two jars changed hands.

Inside, the stove burned with a stillness that had not been there on any previous morning.

"Tonight we do not sleep," the old man said.

His mother nodded once. "Then we do not sleep."

The fire-seed tree swayed in the wind for a long time.

The two jars were opened. The ancestor-ash and the stove-ash — the two colors indistinguishable to the eye.

The old man then drew from within his robe a small cloth packet. Inside it were the two birch pieces from Bai Yin's stored log — thin birch split into slivers and kept safe for hundreds of days.

He placed the two slivers into the fire.

The birch burned slowly, quietly, without a single pop.

No one spoke.

The two families fed the same hearth for the first time in seven hundred years.

The flame burned low, steady, and gold.

It had never burned so steadily before.

That night, A-Shi's mother made seven dishes.

She did not count them in advance. She did not plan them. She simply walked from one end of the courtyard to the other, saw the stove burning, and her hands knew how to cook.

The old man ate slowly.

Each bite, his eyes closed for a moment before opening again.

"Fragrant," he said.

He had never been inside this house before.

His first bite of its food was the first bite he had ever taken in this house.

Xiao Huo leaned against the stove and watched the flames.

Xiao Huo.

The seven-hundredth year.

The first equinox.

Two houses.

One hearth.
