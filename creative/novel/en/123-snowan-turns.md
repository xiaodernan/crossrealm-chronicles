## One Hundred and Twenty-three: Snow'an Turns Back

Saturday of the eighth week after the spring equinox.

Clear all day. The white light of late afternoon touched the courtyard warmly.

Mother-of-Snow'an came to the stove again. She did not speak. She stood before the stove for a long time. In her hand she held a basket. In the basket were two cotton shoes.

She set the shoes on the low bench in front of the stove. No one spoke.

Snow'an stood with her.

Snow'an was smiling slightly.

"You're back," Mother said.

"No," Snow'an said. Very quietly. He looked at the fire differently — not with fear — with calm.

The fire burned still.

The gate opened and shut. The rain came down hard.

Mother-of-Snow'an set all three pairs of shoes on the stove iron.

Mother brought stools.

Four people — Mother, A-Shi, Xiao Huo, Snow'an's mother — sat in front of the stove.

Nobody spoke.

The fire-seed tree was shaking with sand and wind-whipped leaves.

The silence lasted one hour. The stove burned steady. Twice the flame leapt. Each person put one hand forward, warming palms in the light of the fire. The stove burned. Seven hundred years of fire. 炉火 burned still.

Snow'an's mother watched the stove. Her eyes were a little red. She didn't cry. The fire sent its light into her eyes and her eyes became warm.

She spoke at last.

"I came to tell you —"

The flame leapt once.

"I came to tell the fire —"

The world fell silent around her.

"Your house has no outsider; your fire has no outsider; your fire has been kept by a tree; your fire has never died for seven hundred years — your kitchen —"

She paused.

"The whole town has said so — the stove of Bai Yin's house — never goes out."

No one answered.

炉火.
