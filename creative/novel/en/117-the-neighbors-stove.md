## One Hundred and Seventeen: The Neighbor's Stove

The rain at the neighbor's house had not let up.

It rained for fourteen days. The ditch along our gate carved itself deeper. The great flat stone at the corner of the yard wobbled once, twice, and slid halfway into the flow.

Old Li came every day.

He came earlier each time.

Mother did not say anything. She simply told A-Shi to let him stand a while and watch.

The first day he stood for nearly half an hour. The stove burned steadily. He watched the tips of the flames.

The second day he stood the whole morning. Mother made four dishes. Old Li did not touch his chopsticks. He only watched the stove.

The third day he stood closer — one foot from the stove iron — for nearly three full hours.

On the fourth day it rained, and he came early, walking over the path with his shoes soaked.

"I came for the fire," he said.

A-Shi looked at him.

"On my own. I won't ask my six to do it. Just me."

No one said anything.

Old Li stood a little longer. Then he said, "Stove. Tomorrow I come to cook."

His mother smiled.

"Then come," she said.

Old Li nodded.

On the tenth day the sky cleared. The ditch outside the gate ran red-brown with silt.

Old Li came carrying a small iron pot.

He stood at the door a long time. Then he walked in and stood before the stove.

He set the pot on the stove.

No one said a word.

He crouched down, filled the pot with a little water. Then from his pocket he drew a handful of rice — only a few grains — and poured them into the water.

The stove.

It was burning.

The pot stirred — barely a sound.

Old Li stood a little longer. He went out to the porch and stopped there.

"Stove," he said. "I'll come again tomorrow."

No one answered.

A wind swept through the yard. It pushed the gate open.

The sky cleared.

Everyone in the family stood on the porch and watched.

The stove had not gone out.

For the first time in seven hundred years, these people and this stove were waiting together for the neighbor to come.

The stove.

It was burning.

It was calling.
