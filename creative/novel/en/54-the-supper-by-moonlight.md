## Fifty-four: The Supper by Moonlight

Winter came gently.

The night was almost black at its heart, but the candlelight in the clay bowl shone so clearly that the old persimmon's shadow fell across the courtyard like a falling fan. A-Shi sat on the porch with a bowl of rice in his hands.

His mother came out and sat beside him. She said nothing.

She looked toward the sky.

Two figures entered the yard from the far end.

A-Shi lifted his head.

He recognized the old man at once — impossible, but undeniable — his grandfather.

He had yellow-white beard stubble and weathered eyes that had seen everything twice.

He stood and looked at A-Shi.

"Come here," he said.

A-Shi froze. His legs did not want to move.

Then they did.

He walked toward the old man slowly. The moon threw both their shadows long across the courtyard — too long, the way moonlight only shows things when it wants them to linger.

His grandfather smiled and said one name:

"A-Shi."

Then he added:

"Eat with me."

They sat side by side on the stone ledge — the dish of rice between them, the clay bowl warm behind them, the moon just above them.

A-Shi took a small bite. He could not speak. He could not look.

But he ate.

His grandfather did not eat.

His grandfather only held the bowl for a short time, held it as if someone had just lent it to him from very far away.

The air over the yard felt vast and utterly still.

A-Shi stood; he did not move.

He did not stop until the moon crested lower — and his grandfather's shadow disappeared into the glow.

A-Shi looked at the bowl.

Something shone under the sooty base.

When he pressed his finger against it, it was warm.

He did not cry. But his heart stopped, almost for one instant — then unstopped.

From that day he knew:

His grandfather had not gone.

His grandfather had been beside him the entire time.

The fire had not died.

It had never died.
