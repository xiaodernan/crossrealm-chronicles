## One Hundred and Thirty-three: The Third Bowl

Saturday of the seventeenth week after the spring equinox — morning — the weather clear.

Aunt Guo came again. She stood in the yard first — then sat down before the stove — the fire burning steadily.

She brought three things: a brand-new white porcelain bowl, a bag of glutinous rice, and a bundle of new birch.

“The fire has a mouth now, too,” she said.

A-Shi saw the water boiling — he dropped the rice in — the fragrance of rice threaded its way up into the fire itself.

The white bowl went on the iron rim. “For the fire,” Aunt Guo said.

A-Shi pushed the bowl toward the hearth’s mouth — he did not touch the flames — he only set it there.

The fire leapt once.

Aunt Guo watched for a long time. “Seven hundred years, and no one thought to feed it. Seven hundred years.”

A-Shi did not answer.

The three of them sat before the stove — no one spoke.

Uncle Zhou came in — he saw it — drank his own bowl — and left.

The white bowl stayed on the iron rim — no one touched it again.

In the evening, when Aunt Guo came to take the bowl back, the bowl was empty.

She looked at it for a long time — then set it back on the iron rim. “Come and look tomorrow,” she said.

The fire burned.

The fire burned.
