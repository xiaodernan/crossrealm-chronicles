## One Hundred and Twenty-four: The Whole Town and the Fire

Week nine after the spring equinox — Saturday. Sunlight filled the yard.

The yard was full of people.

The first was Zhou, the tofu seller from the end of the village. He stood at the door for a long time.

The second was Lao Guo from next door.

The third was someone nobody recognized.

Then five more — then fifteen — then twenty-five more streamed in until the yard could hardly hold them.

Mother was rushing around at the stove overwhelmed by everyone arriving. Her hands were flying everywhere. Nobody said a word about it. She kept adding water to the pot — slowly, carefully — the pot filled up. The fire burned steadily. The pot was beginning to boil.

A-Shi stood in the corner, watching the stove.

"Don't be afraid," he said.

The whole town fell silent.

Seven hundred years ago, this same town had gathered around this stove the day the first keeper passed away. They had come to see the fire that would outlive every one of them.

They came one by one, filing in.

They did not speak. They looked at the stove. Their faces in the orange light were impossible to read. Every one of them was looking at the fire.

Not one of them left.

The pot boiled over — Mother carried the steaming pot to the long bench — a line formed — a line of the whole town — everyone waited patiently for a bowl.

Even Xiao Huo got one.

Mother stood at the far end of the bench looking at the stove.

The whole town — looking at the stove — nobody spoke — nobody left.

The stove had never gone out.

炉火.
