## Ninety-six: Baiyin

Baiyin came without warning.

She arrived at midday carrying nothing — a single bundle, nothing more, nothing of herself that needed to arrive with her.

The stove saw her immediately. It rose in a welcoming curve — one long, gentle gesture, a wave of gold reaching up toward the ceiling.

A-Shi stopped in the doorway.

His mother was inside, sweeping the floor. Neither of them said anything about Baiyin standing in the threshold with the fire reaching up for her.

Baiyin set her bundle down by the stove — she did not open it.

She simply stood there, watching the fire.

The stove flickered once. A single tongue of flame leapt upward.

She reached out one hand — hovering just above the rim, hovering in the heat — and the stove breathed.

She pulled her hand back slowly.

She turned to A-Shi, and she smiled, very slightly, very carefully.

"The fire remembers you," she said.

He knew it did.

"The fire has always remembered," she said.

He did not know that, until she said it.

His mother brought a bowl of water and set it on the table.

She poured a little of that water into the stove — she had done it every morning for six hundred years — and the fire leapt up, gold and warm, and Baiyin closed her eyes for a moment with the gesture of someone who had been carrying too much for too long.

She drank the water.

Then she stood up.

"I have come to see the fire," she said. "To see it with you, one last time."

"Yes," A-Shi said.

"Tomorrow, I must leave," she said.

He nodded.

She walked to the door, turned back once, and then left.

The stove flickered after she was gone.

In the evening, his mother cooked the usual meal. The fire burned as it had always burned — steady, warm, golden.

But A-Shi understood something now, for the first time.

The fire had never stopped. It had never gone away. It had never needed to be relit, or reminded, or told to stay.

The fire had been here all along. Six hundred years of faithful, patient light, right here in this kitchen.

And now, for reasons he did not yet understand, it had remembered something.

He did not know what yet.

But tomorrow, he would learn.
