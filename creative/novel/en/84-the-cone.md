# The Unchanted Chronicle of Crossrealm: The Keeper of the Anchor Fire

**Genre**: Planar fantasy / dark epic / metafiction
**Adapted from**: Crossrealm Chronicles
**Volume XII**: The Long Journey — Fire and Ink

## Eighty-four: The Coning

On the morning of the Winter Solstice, A-Shi went out to gather wood, and saw one cone on the fire-seed tree.

He drew closer. A small thing, barely the size of his thumb's knuckle, painted a dark umber red.

His mother came to the doorway with a bowl in her hand and saw it too.

"What is that on the tree?"

She said nothing for a long while.

"It is the fire-seed tree's first cone," she said at last. "The sign of the next keeper."

A-Shi said nothing.

"Your great-grandmother's mother used to say: when the fire-seed tree cones for the first time in its life, it is telling the family — the next keeper is on the road."

Her tone did not change. Her eyes went soft.

Snow began to fall.

The stove burned quietly all night.

At some point long past midnight, A-Shi opened his eyes to find a man seated before the stove.

Middle-aged. Thin. A grey robe. His breathing was as steady as the stove's breath.

The man noticed A-Shi was awake. He smiled faintly.

"I have been sent," he said, "to bring the ledger."

A-Shi asked, "Who are you?"

"I am called Guo Ke," the man said. "They call me the Walker. My business is the sixth-hundred-and-second keeper's ledger."

"What ledger?"

He laid a bundle of pages on the table — yellowed, brittle with age, filled with characters nobody living had ever read out loud.

"Every keeper you will ever know in the next book," Guo Ke said, "kept their own ledger."

A-Shi looked down at the paper and felt the weight of ink six hundred years old settling in his hands.

He flipped to the first page.

A single character: 记.

Every page after — the same.

And then, deeper in, his own grandfather's hand appeared — his short-across stroke, light and telling. After that, his father's lighter ink.

And then a blank page.

"This is yours," Guo Ke said.

"What does it want?"

"The next page."

A-Shi looked at the blank. His hand began to tremble without his willing it.

Baiyin had come and gone like weather. His mother was always there but never spoke of it. The tree had coned twice since midwinter. Every detail was running ahead of him now.

"You will receive it when you are ready," Guo Ke said, "not before."

He prepared to leave.

At the gate, he paused.

"Six hundred years," he said. "You are not alone in this. You are only the next bloom of this tree."

He slipped into the snow.

A-Shi stood alone in the doorway.

On the table, the ledger waited — six hundred and one names already written in it.

And one.

His mother joined him in the doorway.

Neither of them spoke.

The Third Six Hundredth Winter passed the way all six hundred years had done — softly.

At first light, by the stove, the tree gave up a second cone.

It was redder, still, than the first.

The keeper's ledger on the table had been waiting six hundred years to close the last page — and was ready to begin the next.

A-Shi sealed it with his hand.

His mother said quietly, "Keeper."

He nodded.

He did not know what came next for him — but the tree knew, and the ledger knew, and the stove knew. He had been a keeper from the moment he first touched it — he simply had not known how to look back.

The dawn broke clean over the ridge. Across it, one lamp lit by another, six hundred years of fire now burned bright enough to see from the stars.

Someone had written it all in.
