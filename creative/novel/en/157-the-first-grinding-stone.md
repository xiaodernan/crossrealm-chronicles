## One Hundred and Fifty-seven: The First Grinding Stone

Monday of the twenty-sixth week after the spring equinox — morning — the sky not fully bright.

His mother sat at the stove — she picked up the pestle — she said to A-Shi: “Today you grind.”

A-Shi looked at the pestle — the pestle belonged to a mill — the mortar was old — its grooves were worn deep by seven hundred years of grinding.

He asked: “What are we grinding?”

His mother said: “Rice — white rice — seven hundred years — this is the first time with new rice.”

A-Shi nodded — he stretched out his hand — his mother passed him the pestle — it went into his hands — he held the handle — he did not dare apply much force.

His mother said: “No need to force it — let time help you.”

A-Shi held it — he did not use force — he turned it slowly — the husk came off the grains — rustle rustle — a very small sound.

A-Shi said: “Did my great-grandfather grind?”

His mother said: “Every generation did.”

A-Shi said: “Then I have ground it too.”

His mother did not laugh — she nodded — she waited until A-Shi finished one grain — she showed him the ground rice — it was done.

A-Shi set the pestle down — the fire burned.

His mother said: “Good — next time you grind yourself.”

A-Shi looked at the fire — he did not speak — the fire burned.

The fire burned.
