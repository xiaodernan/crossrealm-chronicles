## One Hundred and Forty-three: The Word on the Card

Sunday of the twenty-third week after the spring equinox — morning — the sky very white.

A-Shi had not spoken — Aunt Guo had not opened her mouth — his mother had not moved either.

The fire burned.

A-Shi held his bowl of thin porridge — he looked at the card again — he had remembered the character.

His mother said: “Done — eat now.”

He finished — he put the bowl down — the fire burned.

Aunt Guo said: “Remembered it?”

A-Shi nodded — he did not open his mouth — the fire burned.

His mother went out — she took a sieve — she came back holding lotus seeds — she set them on the stove — she did not look up.

The fire burned.

Aunt Guo said: “Now that you’re fed — I’ll show you the second one.”

A-Shi stopped — he lifted his head — the fire burned.

Aunt Guo drew out a stack of cards — she counted — she counted thirteen — she turned one over with her thumb — the character was different.

She turned it over — this was the same character A-Shi had just learned — A-Shi saw it at a glance.

Her mother said nothing — the fire burned.

A-Shi said: “Two characters — one above the fire — one beneath.”

Aunt Guo did not answer — she did not look up — she gathered the cards — she put them away — she said: “Squat and look.”

A-Shi squatted in front of the stove — the fire lit him — he counted the fire — he said nothing — his mother watched the fire — Aunt Guo watched the fire too — the fire burned.

The fire burned.
