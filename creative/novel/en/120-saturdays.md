## One Hundred and Twenty: Saturdays

Saturday morning began under a light rain — not the kind that comes in sheets, but the kind that hangs in the air and clings gently to the eaves and the needles of the fire-seed tree. The yard was cooler than it had been for days. But the stove burned steadily.

His mother was cooking — cabbage and tofu. The pan hissed and sighed.

A-Shi sat in front of the stove, watching the fire.

Xiao Huo sat beside him doing the same.

His mother walked to the stove, walked back to the kitchen.

The gate rattled softly.

A-Shi stood.

A small figure stood in the doorway — young shoulders, small bag on his back, a grey jacket, brand-new cloth shoes.

It was Snow'an.

He did not speak.

He stood in the yard looking at the fire-seed tree. Each layer of leaves was larger now — summer was coming. The green was beginning to shift from cool to warm gold.

He walked into the kitchen.

He sat in front of the stove.

The firelight lit his face softly, from within.

He watched the fire for a long time. Then he slowly got up and reached out one small hand toward the stove iron. He pressed his hand against the iron edge.

The iron was warm.

His hand did not get burned.

The stove burned steady.

He left.

He returned on Sunday the following week.

Then the week after that.

The fourth week he did not come.

The fifth week, he came carrying a small oil jar. He set it on the low table in the yard. Some of the villagers had started to suspect something — that this boy came every single Saturday, sat silently by the fire, and left without speaking.

A-Shi told his mother, "He keeps coming."

"He keeps coming," Mother said.

The fire-seed tree swayed.

"He won't keep coming forever," Xiao Huo said.

"He will," A-Shi said.

The tree shook in the breeze.

A-Shi and Xiao Huo and Mother all understood — every Saturday, the boy would come — sit — watch the fire — and leave — without saying a word.

Sometimes Mother would slip him a bowl of rice.

The stove burned.

Seven hundred years had passed.

The stove still burned.
