## Eighty-nine: New Fire

Xiao Huo's first month at A-Shi's house was spent on a single task.

A-Shi set him the same question every morning:

"Watch the stove."

Xiao Huo sat at the hearth and watched. For the first few days, when A-Shi asked what he saw, he said:

"The fire moves."

A-Shi asked:

"Why does it move?"

The boy considered this for a long time.

"…Because someone is in it."

A-Shi nodded.

A month later he asked the same question again. Xiao Huo thought for a while and said:

"There isn't just one person in it — there are many, many people. Taking turns."

A-Shi smiled and did not press him.

Two weeks after that, Xiao Huo added:

"They are all like me in there — everyone tending the fire, everyone who stands before a stove like this, everyone who ever was — like everyone who has ever spent this long in front of a stove like this, in any house, in any year."

A-Shi laughed quietly to himself.

"And what did it call you?" he asked.

The boy thought carefully.

"It did not name me," Xiao Huo said. "It only nodded. Many times."

A-Shi did not ask his mother what that meant. His mother only smiled. She did not explain.

Some days later, A-Shi brought out the small black stone from the wooden box his mother had saved — the one brought up to him by someone from Grandma Bai's house, a fragment of the six-hundred-year-old coal — and set it down in front of Xiao Huo.

Xiao Huo looked at it. He did not blink.

"Is this from Grandma Bai?" he asked.

A-Shi nodded.

The boy was silent a long moment.

"Then this stone can tell you one thing," Xiao Huo said. "Grandma Bai has never stopped for six hundred years."

A-Shi asked, "How do you know?"

Xiao Huo looked at him steadily.

"Because I call her by her name," he said.

"What do you call her?"

The boy lowered his voice.

"I call her Grandma Bai," he said. "Even when I speak to her myself — I always say Grandma Bai."

The stove flared — a sensation stronger than anything A-Shi had felt in many years.

His mother came through from the kitchen. She saw Xiao Huo and stopped. A faint, almost transparent light clung to the edges of the boy's shoulders — something A-Shi had seen before in Baiyin's presence.

She said nothing. She went back.

Some time later, A-Shi asked Xiao Huo:

"Who is Grandma Bai?"

The boy did not hesitate.

"Grandma Bai is the fire," Xiao Huo said. "Grandma Bai is this stove. Grandma Bai is the six hundred years of keepers — every single one of them."

A-Shi's eyes began to sting.

His mother came closer, laid one hand gently on his shoulder.

The stove burned steadily.

A-Shi opened the ledger — the one with all six hundred years of signatures, the empty page at the very end — and placed it on the table before Xiao Huo.

"This is the next book," he said. "When it is filled, this stove will know what to do next."

Xiao Huo nodded solemnly.

"Next time you write in it," A-Shi said, "write your own name."

Xiao Huo looked at the page for a long while.

He did not write.

He only turned and looked at the fire.

"Not today," he said. "Another day."

The stove rose very high.

The fire-seed tree outside changed its needles — almost all of them turned deepest crimson.

On the far ridge, the smoke from the beacon was gone. In its place the whole valley was burning quietly — from the ever-burning lamp, from every stove in every household, from

A-Shi's stove, from Xiao Huo's new fire.

Six hundred years became the seven-hundredth year.

New fire.

New keeper.
