## One Hundred and Sixty-one: Raising the Anchor

Monday of the twenty-seventh week after the spring equinox — morning — the sky not yet fully bright.

A-Shi woke — the fire burned — on the stove there was something he had never seen — a small anchor stone — a small rock — with three characters carved on it — but he could not make them out.

The stone mortar roused itself too — it gave a single sound — as if someone had struck inside it — his mother was not at the stove — she was only in the room.

A-Shi picked up the stone — he held it close to the fire — the fire burned — the characters lit up.

On it was written — “Raising the Anchor.”

He did not speak — he looked at the fire — the fire burned.

He set it on the table — he looked at the stove again — his mother pushed the door open — she stood in the doorway — she saw it too.

His mother said: “The anchor is rising.”

A-Shi said: “When did it start?”

His mother said: “Seven hundred years.”

A-Shi did not answer — the fire burned.

The fire burned.

The fire burned.
