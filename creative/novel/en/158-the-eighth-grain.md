## One Hundred and Fifty-eight: The Eighth Grain

Monday of the twenty-sixth week after the spring equinox — morning — the sky was already half-bright.

The rice jar at the stove held new rice — A-Shi picked one grain after another — he counted seven, and stopped.

His mother said: “Finish counting, then call me.”

A-Shi did not answer — he counted one more — eight.

He put the grains in a bowl — he looked at the bowl — the fire burned.

His mother walked over carrying half a bowl of congee — she did not speak — she set the bowl in front of A-Shi — the fire burned.

A-Shi looked at the grains in the bowl — he reached out — he ate one grain — eight.

His mother stood behind him — she did not speak — the fire burned.

A-Shi ate another grain — nine.

He did not stop — he kept eating — ten — eleven — twelve — until only one grain was left in the bowl — the fire burned.

A-Shi set the last grain back on the stove — he looked at his mother — his mother looked at the fire — the fire burned.

He said: “Keep the last one.”

His mother nodded — she said: “Our rule about leaving rice isn't because we are poor — it is so that the next time the fire burns, there is someone who remembers what the first bowl meant.”

The fire burned.

His mother came over — she picked up that grain — she placed it in her palm — she held her hand out — A-Shi held his hand out too — his mother placed the grain in his palm — A-Shi closed his hand around it.

The fire burned.

The fire burned.
