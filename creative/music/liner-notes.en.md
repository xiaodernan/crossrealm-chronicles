# 《Ember Low Hum》(anchor-fire low murmur) — liner notes for the 25

> English mirror of `creative/music/liner-notes.md` — what the 25 tracks are *for*. Written for everyone who owns this record — or who is about to press a key somewhere inside the game. Not a playlist. An explanation.
> **How to read**: three sentences per track — the stage (what it films), the memory (what scene it escorts), the realm (where it resonates). Some tracks never sound in full in the game — they are not “a song,” they are “the airflow of the game” — e.g. 06 Campfire Ember, 25 Ember Low Hum.

---

## Disc 1 · Anchor (Anchor) — where the hearth grows its first light

**01 Anchor Pulse · the heart of the planes / the Hub**
The anchor is neither god nor door. It is simply where the heartbeat found the middle of the world. This is the heartbeat’s raw note — a weld across the film; that E2→A2 low triangle lives in the bones of all 25 tracks. Whoever listens through this one first knows how every later “road” finds its rhythm.

**02 Three Roads · the Hub exit**
Before you go, three entries — left, right, straight — each with its own weather. This refuses to spoil which one you should pick. The first principle of *Crossrealm Chronicles*: **the door never chooses for you what to walk on — it just makes the road appear.**

**03 Grey Mist Waltz · the Greywood**
No thiefs in the wilderness. Everything and nothing — foxes, wind, and far too many footprints pressed in snow, never quite breaking it. A tiny-step waltz: every cycle runs once and hides one classical counterpoint inside — the unfinished “no one ever said aloud in the mist.” Listen long enough and you’ll know the shape of the fog.

**04 Stone Ring Chant · the Sovereign Stone Ring POI**
A hummed chant — not for people. For the stones. It rhymes with the prologue line: “One Realm to bind them all… the ring broke; the stone ring remains.” The octaves in this track are older than the Ring — and younger — because they were the ones remembered.

## Disc 2 · Campfire Ember — where the most people sit

**05 Shadow Warden · Boss 1**
The world didn’t start with a fight. The fight began because the door happened to open inside a shadow. The track arrives the way a guard opens the door for you — opening a door carries no excitement, but it never hesitates. The first time you press F to parry, you’ll understand: **what you’re fighting isn’t it — it’s the fact that it let you get this far.**

**06 Campfire Ember · every campfire**
… no beat, no melodic spine, nothing but the harmony of fire working. The only track in the whole game that never plays in full — it only sounds inside that one-inch mist at a save point; every successful save lets it quietly grow one more note. Collect one hundred saves and you hear the whole song.

**07 Floating Candle · the Magic Realm**
A floating candle has one job: to warn, in advance, that this is somewhere that floats. The figure here belongs to the apprentice’s feather-note family — octave leap in, minor-sixth fall out, like the candle peering out of the tower and shrinking back.

**08 Broom Ring Race · the Flying Hoop POI**
6/8, double-time, sly contents — this is a track written for children in the Magic Realm, its rhythm out of the hourglass and the flame alternating as juggling toys. **The metronome hears “hover,” players hears “adventure” — and the children on the inside hear “the next page”** — three readings, one meter, and the writer declines to say which is correct.

## Disc 3 · Upside Chaos — the three deepest rooms in the Timeline

**09 Endurance Log · the Murphy shelf POI**
Murphy’s letter wasn’t written — it fell off the shelf on its own, one page at a time. The paper hangs, turns, flattens — every time Murphy speaks, somewhere in the ash one fibre moves between the strokes of the words. This track’s page-sound carries more of the weight of the words than paper ever could.

**10 Murphy’s Stay · the Murphy shelf POI**
Duller and stiller than the previous one. *Stay* — stopping isn’t always “waiting,” it could also be “keep.” The only address in the whole record where the listener can’t sit still and can’t stand up — tuned to “every second you are here is every second Murphy is smiling.”

**11 Miller’s Hourglass · time-freeze trigger**
Free meter, 72→160 BPM ramp. The hourglass isn’t running — it is dancing *between* “leaking” and “not leaking.” **Every grain is proof that time hasn’t decided to move yet.**

**12 Singularity Herald · the Boss**
Enters from an extreme low D; exits into an even lower G — never once lifting its face upward. You have exactly one way: down. But no note in this track is forever stuck at the bottom — **one of them raised its eyes before it sank.**

**13 1983 Loop · the broadcast tower**
Loop + wow tape hiss: “the last sound the previous cycle ever uttered.” This isn’t 1983 — 1983 is just a tag for how long one cycle managed to survive. Every twist in this track **contains one second of 1983’s air**, without naming where that air belongs.

**14 Upside Spores · the Upside overlay**
Spores floating — full-register, dense and shredded; as an overlay, its one job is to hold the scene, not the story. Those who’ve heard this track know: **the plot can forget what spores look like — the spores stay.**

**15 11:06 Bell · the Crackstone Clocktower**
The bell isn’t built to tell time — it is built to announce “someone else has come into the factory.” Every stroke is a “once more — someone entered.” The distance between how nobody may watch the tower and how the bell-ringer looks at it is the distance between “not yet” and “already inside.”

**16 Spiral Count · the deep shaft**
Free meter + spoken countdown. This isn’t singing — it is the act of counting, by itself. Anyone who counted thirty seconds — or listened to it for thirty — never figures out what counting is for: but the shaft is only waiting for that one suspended second when you stop halfway.

**17 Speakeasy Tariff · the Chaos Fringe**
Jazz fifths + low sax noir — the only place at the Fringe where “a rule nobody sees” still holds. The tariff isn’t a tax — it is the Fringe’s last working rule about obeying rules. **When the sax kicks in, the disorder doubles as a city.**

**18 Chaos Overlord · the final Boss**
Ice crystals colliding with lava — the only track on the record that needs two instruments to fight each other. The fight isn’t won by anyone; **the fight is a rite — the rite is a separation — and after the separation each goes on with its own world: lava keeps rising, ice keeps sinking.**

## Disc 4 · Echoes & Epilogue — the endless prequel

**19 Western Mirror · the Fringe’s old days**
Harmonica + strings — this is the past life of one room: the mirror smashed again and again, replaced again and again, and every replacement held a face not quite the same. **The person in the mirror is becoming the next mirror.**

**20 Samurai Gate · the samurai remnant**
Shakuhachi — the highest honour this remnant receives; even he doesn’t know it. He didn’t come to fight. He only stays to keep “the aesthetics of never saying sorry.” **The gate opens itself; he sits inside it; no one has ever dared to measure his sword.**

**21 Neon Alley Rain · the cyber-neon district**
Synth arp at 110 BPM — rain on props, props glowing on you. This section is a secret locked inside rain: **the door doesn’t keep rain out; the person inside does. You only see which scene you’re the star of when you’re walking in it.**

**22 Starport Cantina · the starport lanes**
Jazz fifths at 98 BPM — the starport’s rules differ from chaos: chaos runs on disorder, the starport runs on “best deal.” This isn’t a cheerful track — **it is the track where every deal closed happily, and every deal remembers exactly how it was wrong.**

**23 Full Resonance · 109/109 unlock**
Every motif in the series layered — the whole fragment, prologue to finale. This track contains **every motive** you ever heard; and **every motive** you ever missed. 109/109 isn’t “everything” — it is finally admitting there are things that never needed to be heard.

**24 Infinity Walker · roll-of-credits**
The anchor theme returns to the pulse — the credit roll takes “endless” off the page: **endless means it keeps playing by itself. No Esc stops it.**

**25 Ember Low Hum · the after-completion coda · same band as endingFlame**
E2→A2 triangle waves rising and falling slowly + 41.2 Hz infrasonic bed — the slowest, deepest, unsynthesizable track of the record. It beats in the same frequency as the “the Anchor still burns” after-completion state. It is the source of *Interlude I · A Letter from the Fire* — “every time you press E, this letter runs one line further.” It beats in the same frequency as the fog retreating one inch in *Interlude II · The Keeper’s Reply*; it resonates with that moment in *Interlude III · Lin’s Reply to the Keeper* — the fog retreating an inch is your press of E; and together with *Interlude IV · The Reply of the One Who Came After*, it is the cadence of a letter that keeps being written after you put the book down — the one that ends “turn it over.” The only voice band in the entire companion short *The Keeper’s Day*. **This track has no ending — it only leaves the low hum halfway in the air; a story that walks by its own rhythm needs no ending.**

---

**—— The flame refuses to go out early; the story refuses to close early.**

*(These liner notes are written for everyone who ever stopped to listen. The fire did not go out, and neither did the lights on the poster wall — they were only waiting for you to come back in.)*
